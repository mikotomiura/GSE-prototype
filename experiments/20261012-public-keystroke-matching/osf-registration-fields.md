## Study information

**Title**

How far do processing steps applied to public keystroke logs protect against matching logs to texts?

**Description**

Keystroke logs of writing are released publicly with processing intended to hide the texts, such as masking letters and digits. Because the texts often exist elsewhere, an attacker can try to match a released log to its text among candidate texts. This study measures how far release forms (the masking used in practice and alternative forms: key categories, dropped position and word count, merged separators, rounded times, coarsened segment values, suppression, substitution and permutation) protect against matching attackers that know the processing, on the public KLiCKe corpus of English argumentative essays and on the public ENJA15 corpus of Japanese translations typed with an input method. Accuracy is reported against the chance level 1/N and against an attacker that uses only total length, together with how far standard process measures of writing research remain computable after processing. No pass or fail criterion is set. The full plan is in the attached preregistration.en.md, with all parameter values in config.json and the random seed in SEED.

**Hypotheses**

No confirmatory hypothesis is tested; the study is descriptive. The research questions and the predictions written before any data were acquired are in the sections "Questions" and "Predictions" of preregistration.en.md. For ENJA15, the word "exceeds" is used only when the lower bounds of the 95% intervals of the accuracy differences to 1/N and to the length-only attacker are both above 0 (section "Questions", item 4).

## Design plan

**Study type**

Observational study: secondary analysis of existing public data (no new data are collected).

**Blinding**

No blinding is involved in this study.

**Study design**

Each evaluated keystroke log is processed into each release form and matched by each attacker against N candidate texts (the true text and N − 1 decoys of the same prompt), for N = 10, 100 and 1,000, on full texts and on windows of k non-empty segments (k = 5, 10, 20, 50). Attackers that need training are trained on the writers of the two prompts not being evaluated (the four prompts are split into two pairs, and the roles are swapped). Release forms, attackers, segment rules, alignment, candidates and reported quantities are in the sections "Release forms", "Segments", "Alignment", "Attackers" and "Candidates, scope and numbers" of preregistration.en.md.

**Randomization**

All random draws (the split of prompts, the 500-writer evaluation sample, decoys, window starts, random processing, the negative control, bootstrap resamples) use a seed derived from the fixed string in SEED with the formula in preregistration.en.md (section "Candidates, scope and numbers", row "Random numbers").

## Sampling plan

**Existing data**

Registration prior to accessing the data. The KLiCKe logs, texts and scores have not been downloaded or opened. One ENJA15 file was downloaded earlier for an unrelated purpose and only its event types were counted; it is not used (section "What was seen before this registration was fixed").

**Explanation of existing data**

The data are public corpora. Before registration, only the documentation of the corpora, the structure of the KLiCKe file folder (names and counts) and the description of a related Kaggle competition were read. The procedures were checked on synthetic logs generated by our code, without human data (section "Check on synthetic logs").

**Data collection procedures**

KLiCKe (Tian, Crossley & Van Waes, 2025): the keystroke logs, essays and human scores of the argumentative writing task, acquired after the researcher decides to proceed in light of the authors' reply to an inquiry; the demographics file is not acquired. ENJA15 (Carl, Aizawa & Yamada, 2016): from-scratch translation sessions from the TPR-DB repository at a fixed SVN revision, after the license and terms of use are confirmed. The public KeyTAR model (Qiu et al., 2026) from Zenodo record 17254163. Versions and SHA-256 of all files are recorded at acquisition (section "Data").

**Sample size**

All writers of KLiCKe after exclusion (4,992 writers in 4 prompts as reported, recounted after acquisition); the main tables use 500 writers (125 per prompt) drawn with the seed. All from-scratch translation sessions of ENJA15 after exclusion.

**Sample size rationale**

The sample is the whole public corpus. The 500-writer sample is set by the computational cost of the reference attacker (KeyTAR); if it cannot be run on the local GPU, only its rows use a smaller sample (section "Attackers").

**Stopping rule**

Not applicable (existing data; all eligible records are used).

## Variables

**Manipulated variables**

Release form (R0 to R7 and their parameters), attacker (A0, A1, A1', A2, A2-b, A3, A4), number of candidates N, and scope (full text or window size k). Definitions in preregistration.en.md and config.json.

**Measured variables**

Whether the true text is chosen (with ties counted as 1/m), the rank of the true text, alignment costs, and the 10 process measures of Table 5 of Tian et al. (2025) computed from each release form (section "Utility").

**Indices**

Accuracy, its multiple of 1/N, its gain over the length-only attacker A0, the strongest adaptive attacker per form, contrasts between adjacent forms, and Spearman correlations and ratios of the process measures to their unprocessed values (sections "Reported quantities" and "Utility").

## Analysis plan

**Statistical models**

No statistical model is fitted for inference. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals with 2,000 resamples, with writers as units stratified by prompt (KLiCKe) and translators as units (ENJA15, stratified or not depending on whether translators and source texts are crossed). The strongest attacker's interval takes the maximum within each resample.

**Transformations**

Processing into release forms, segment rules and alignment are defined in preregistration.en.md. The active writing window is trimmed from the first key press to the last event that changes the text, in every form.

**Inference criteria**

No significance test and no pass or fail criterion. Results are reported as estimates with intervals. For question 4 (ENJA15), "exceeds" requires both interval lower bounds above 0.

**Data exclusion**

Writers with a missing log, text or score, and writers whose text has fewer than 50 words; ENJA15 sessions with a missing or empty translation, or without Japanese input events. Counts are reported (section "Data").

**Missing data**

Cells with too few valid writers or windows are not reported and are counted. Candidates without an alignment path have infinite cost; ties among all-infinite candidates count as 1/N; these cases are counted (sections "Alignment" and "Candidates, scope and numbers").

**Exploratory analysis**

Secondary quantities (top-5 accuracy, mean reciprocal rank, per-writer accuracy percentiles, sensitivity to the alignment penalty, an open-world diagnostic, a negative control and a bias check of true windows) are listed in preregistration.en.md and are reported as secondary.
