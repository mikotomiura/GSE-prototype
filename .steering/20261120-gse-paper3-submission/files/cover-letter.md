To the Editor-in-Chief, User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction

Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript "Per-person sequence models of keystroke behaviour: what remains of the gain against stronger
comparisons" for consideration as a research article in User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction.

Whether to keep a separate model for each user is a recurring decision in user modelling, and reported per-person
gains are often measured against a weak shared model. Using public co-writing (CoAuthor) and programming (CS1)
keystroke data, the manuscript scores per-person hidden Markov models on each writer's later sessions against two
baselines: a shared model and the writer's own first-order Markov chain. A per-person model that beats a shared model of
the same structure loses to the writer's own chain, and with a stronger structure and shrinkage chosen on other
writers the remainder is small, depends on the smoothing and does not show stable per-person differences. From these
results the manuscript derives an evaluation practice for group-level decisions on per-person modelling, which I
believe fits the journal's readership because it concerns how user models should be compared before personalization
is adopted.

Related manuscripts. Two manuscripts by the same author are under review at the Journal of Educational Data Mining.
"The 30-second signal changes of a hand-set keystroke state estimator before code-writing stalls stayed inside a
preregistered equivalence band against short pauses" (submission 1484; preprint https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24026330)
studies an estimator I designed; the symbolization used here reuses its front end (five keystroke features over the
preceding 30 seconds, combined and smoothed), and that manuscript also uses the CS1 data and, in an exploration,
CoAuthor. "What moves with the pause definition and the adjustment: a multiverse diagnostic of pause and stall labels
in keystroke logs" (submission 1486) uses CoAuthor and the 2021 CS1 data among four datasets. The questions, designs
and results of the three manuscripts differ, and no result is shared: neither of the others fits sequence models to the
data.

Status of the analyses. Every analysis and check ran under conditions committed to the author's version control before
it ran; these are not third-party registrations. Online Resource 1 (S8) lists their order and marks the analyses
decided after seeing results. The code, the conditions and the aggregate and synthetic results are archived at
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24395569.

Data. No new data were collected. CoAuthor is public. The CS1 files were used with the permission of their authors.
