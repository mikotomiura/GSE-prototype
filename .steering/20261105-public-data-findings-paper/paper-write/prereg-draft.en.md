# Preregistration draft v8 (paper 1; the version before freezing on OSF; 2026-11-06)

> This document is the preregistration of an analysis that checks, on writers not yet opened, whether the rise in the run-up of an estimate made from keystrokes falls within the equivalence band in a matched comparison. The rules are implemented in `experiments/20261105-findings-paper1-prefreeze/scripts/confirm_p1.py` (before freezing, it was run through to the end without using the writers not yet opened; Appendix C). At freezing, the SHA-256 of `confirm-manifest.sha256`, the list of verification files that includes this document, is written in the OSF registration. After freezing, this document is not rewritten, and any change is recorded as a deviation.
>
> The decision is fixed at the values of the first completed run on the holdout that passed verification; the handling of discrepancies found after freezing and the order of precedence follow P11.

## P0 What is tested, and how it will be worded

- Contribution: In research that infers states from behavioural logs and intervenes, reports that "the signal rose in the run-up to an event" can be evaluated without matching the comparison partner. For example, Barron et al. (UIST 2026 [V413]) split every 30 s window by whether there is a query to AI in the next 60 s, and predict it. Within the range searched, no report was found that compared, matched, against pauses that did not become events (the range searched and the terms are in the literature record attached to the OSF registration, `docs/literature/20261105-public-data-findings-novelty.md`). This registration checks, on writers not yet opened, whether the rise in the run-up falls within the equivalence band in a matched comparison (the run-up to a stall versus the run-up to a short pause that did not become a stall) (H1; the main claim). Secondarily, in an unmatched comparison of windows outside long pauses within writers (H2), it examines how much the rise falls when re-matched by work phase. H3 is a description with standard keystroke quantities and flexible learners.

- Multiplicity: The paper's main claim rests on H1 alone. H2 is read as a separate question, and no multiplicity correction is made between hypotheses. H3 is descriptive. The 2 signals and the 2 values of ε within H1 are supported only when all are branch 1 (read as an intersection; the estimate of the false-equivalence rate is in P10). Branches 3 and 5 (a difference beyond the band) are limited to description per signal, ε, and learner, and the fact that any one of them is so is not claimed as the discovery of a difference.
- The main sentence closes on H1 alone (if supported: "In the CS1 holdout, the rise in the run-up in the matched comparison fell within the band [0.45, 0.55]"). The contrast between the matched comparison and the unmatched comparison is written as a secondary story, read as a pair with H2. H1 and H2 are two comparisons that differ, besides the way of matching, also in the reference time (u and w), the stratum (H1 is the no-event stratum only; H2 does not restrict it), and the set of negatives, so the paired sentence is a description that lays the two results side by side and does not attribute the difference to the way of matching alone.
  - H1 supported × H2 branch 1: "In the matched comparison, inside the band; the rise in the unmatched comparison fell by half or more when re-matched by work phase."
  - H1 supported × H2 branches 2 and 3: "In the matched comparison, inside the band; in the unmatched comparison there was a rise. With the specified re-matching it fell by less than half (branch 2) / how much it falls with re-matching was undecidable (branch 3)."
  - H1 supported × H2 branch 4: Report only the equivalence in the matched comparison, and do not claim the contrast with the unmatched comparison.
  - When H1 is not supported: Do not pair it with the H2 result; report each separately with the wording of its branch.
- Order of wording when the H1 branches split by signal or ε (an intersection, so not supported): If there is a signal/ε in branch 3 or 5, name it and write "There is a difference beyond the band (descriptive)." If there is none, list branches 2 and 4, degeneracy, and insufficient precision by signal and ε, and write "Equivalence could not be claimed." When only one of the two values of ε is branch 1, write "Equivalence could not be claimed, depending on the handling of ties." When branches 3 and 5 appear in different signals, write the direction per signal.

## P2 Question, theoretical position, and band

- If planning enters serially before keystroke pauses, the way of typing changes in the run-up to long stops, and the estimate can rise. If planning proceeds in parallel with keystroking, signs are unlikely to appear in the run-up. Stops longer than 30 s can include not only planning and execution but also time outside the assignment. The H1 result can agree with either of these two views, so the result is not written as supporting either (it is not a test of theory but a check of the evaluation design).
- The band [0.45, 0.55] was set conservatively after seeing the results of KLiCKe and CoAuthor. It was not derived from a conversion. As a guide to its meaning for readers, AUC 0.55 corresponds, for normal distributions with equal variance, to a small difference in means of about 0.18 times the standard deviation, and the hit rate at a threshold that holds false firings to 10% stays at 13.5% (computation 1). In an example converted, after the band was set, into a threshold-based trigger on seen writers, most firings fell on pauses that did not become stalls, at AUC 0.55 and even at 0.70 (numbers in Appendix D). It has not been shown either that above 0.55 it can be used as a trigger, or that 0.55 is the boundary of whether it can be used. The lower bound of the band, 0.45, was set symmetrically because reversing the sign gives the same conversion.

## P4 Stalls and controls (matched comparison)

- Stalls: For each onset (t0; Appendix A1), take u, and use only those with G(u) of 30 s or more. Look at onsets in time order and count only the first onset with a given u. If u ≥ s at that onset, exclude it; if condition A is true at u (net production in the window (u − W, u] is less than 1), exclude it. Do not replace it with a later onset with the same u.
- Controls: The starts u of pauses of 2 s or more and less than 30 s (the pause length is up to the next character input) of the same writer (collected across assignments). Exclude those that fall in the interval [s − 30 s, t0] of any onset in the same assignment (including onsets that did not become stall rows). Exclude those with condition A true at u (the same function and the same W as for stalls). For each stall, draw up to 5 without replacement from candidates of the same boundary type and the same stratum (the same control being selected for another stall is allowed). The control population is therefore "short pauses that fall neither in the 30 s run-up to a stall nor inside a stall", and is also determined by proximity to onsets that come later (the stall side has no equivalent proximity exclusion).
- Boundary type: whether the keystroke at u is a newline. Stratum: if there is a run (`run`), paste (`paste`), or switch (`switch`) in [u − 30 s, u], it is in the event stratum (no information after u is used). The main decision is in the no-event stratum. The event stratum is descriptive.
- Description within the pause: Because the stratum is determined only from information at or before u, some stalls in the main stratum also include a run, paste, or switch within the pause (from u to the next character input) (the proportion in the 319 seen people is in Appendix C). That proportion (the set that falls in the event stratum in the version that takes the stratum window up to the end of the pause) and the distribution of the number of key presses within the pause (key presses that are not characters, such as deletions and undos) are reported for stalls and controls.
- Exclusions: u less than 60 s from the block start; no estimate row, or a non-numeric one, at or before u or at or before u − 30 s; assignments that hit the upper limit of replay's time span (the number is `span_too_long_days` in the conversion's `build_summary.json`); writers with less than 10 min of observation. All excluded counts are reported (including the numbers of stall and control candidates excluded by condition A).
- Fixed thresholds: The lower bound of 2 s for controls and H3's "number of intervals of 2 s or more and of 10 s or more" are fixed for comparability with the previous experiment (versions adapted to an individual's typing speed are examined via θ in the stall definition and the burst sensitivity analysis).

## P5 Signals and quantities

- Main (H1, H2): `d_p_stuck(t)`, `d_ewma_x(t)` (the difference between the latest estimate row at or before t and the latest row at or before t − 30 s). For H1, t = u; for H2, t = w. Secondary: the levels `p_stuck(t)`, `ewma_x(t)` (descriptive).
- H3 quantities: From the key presses in the 30 s at or before t (characters, space, newline, backspace. The 2019 edition cannot separate Backspace and Delete. Others are excluded), 9 quantities: the number of key presses; the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of inter-keystroke intervals (those exceeding 50 ms); the numbers of intervals of 2 s or more and of 10 s or more; the backspace ratio; the mean burst length (number of key presses, cut by intervals of 2 s or more); and character key presses per minute. Each level, and its difference from the same quantity at t − 30 s (from the key presses in (t − 60 s, t − 30 s]), give 18.
- Missing values: Rows are not excluded. Quantities that cannot be obtained are set missing (quantiles for windows with no intervals; the backspace ratio and burst for windows with no key presses; a difference is missing if either side is missing). The number of key presses is kept as a quantity even when it is 0–2. Whether windows with fewer than 3 key presses or with no intervals occurred in the 30 s at or before u, in the 30 s before that, or in both, is counted over stall and control rows and reported.

## P6 Statistic and decision (H1)

- Statistic: The AUC over all pairs of stall × drawn control (duplicates counted once) within writer × boundary type × stratum, pooled over all writers. A pair scores 1 if stall − control ≥ ε, 0.5 if |stall − control| < ε, and 0 if stall − control ≤ −ε. When ε = 0, it scores 1 if the difference is positive, 0.5 if exactly equal, and 0 if negative (an exact tie is 0.5 under either ε).
- ε: two values, 0.001 and 0. 0.001 is a convention set as the lower bound below which a difference between probabilities on 0–1 is not read as determining the ranking; it is not derived from the resolution of the estimate output (the output is finer than 0.001). Because it was set after the leak, so that ε does not pull toward equivalence, being branch 1 also with ε = 0 is made a requirement of H1.
- Interval: the 95% interval from resampling writers (2,000 iterations; percentile: the int(0.025 k)-th and the min(k − 1, int(0.975 k))-th in ascending order; non-numeric iterations are excluded).
- Instrument degeneracy: A signal whose tie proportion at ε = 0.001 exceeds 50% is "undecidable because the instrument degenerated" (the same decision is applied to the ε = 0 branch). Report the tie proportion, the proportion with equal values at u and u − 30 s, the quantiles of |value|, and the proportions of stalls and controls whose estimate level is at the floor or ceiling (`p_stuck` below 0.02 or above 0.98).
- Estimation target: the AUC of the pairs, pooled over all writers, built from the drawn controls; writers with more stalls and writers for whom more controls could be drawn weigh more (the weighting is shown by the effective number of writers reported alongside, and a writer-equal version is in P9). The interval is a writer resampling with the control draw held fixed, and does not include the variability of the draw.
- Precision guard: If the interval width exceeds 0.10, no branch is applied and it is "undecidable because of insufficient precision".
- Branches (for each signal and each ε; interval [lo, hi]):
  1. lo ≥ 0.45 and hi ≤ 0.55 → equivalent (it can be said that there is no rise larger than this band).
  2. lo ≤ 0.55 < hi → undecidable (upper side).
  3. lo > 0.55 → there is a rise beyond the band (as an overall ranking).
  4. lo < 0.45 ≤ hi → undecidable (lower side).
  5. hi < 0.45 → reverse direction beyond the band.
- H1 is supported when both of the 2 signals are branch 1 under both of the 2 values of ε (since it is an intersection, each signal's 95% interval is used as it is. Nominally this corresponds to a TOST at one-sided 2.5%. The calibration values on seen writers are in P10).
- Reported alongside: the difference from 0.5 (whether the lower bound of the interval exceeds 0.5; the wording is "departs from 0.5"). Weighting (the proportion of pairs held by the top 10% of writers; the effective number of writers). Quantiles of per-writer AUCs. A threshold table (with the 80th, 90th, and 95th percentiles of each writer's drawn controls as thresholds, the proportion of stall run-ups that exceed it and the proportion of controls that exceeded it; the median over writers and the pooled ratio).

## P7 Unmatched comparison (H2)

- Set: windows within writers outside long pauses (not all windows). The positive targets are all stall u's of P4 (regardless of stratum). Windows in the run-up to long pauses that fell outside P4 because of condition A and the like (pauses of 30 s or more not counted as stalls) are included among the negatives. The number of those negative windows is reported, and the version that excludes them from the negatives is a sensitivity analysis in P9.

- Windows: For each work block, take the window end w from the block start + 60 s, in steps of 5 s, up to the block end − 60 s. The window is [w − 30 s, w].
- Positives: windows with a stall's u (P4) in (w, w + 60 s]. Negatives: all others.
- Exclusions: windows whose w lies between the u of a pause of 30 s or more (including those that did not become P4 stalls) and the resumption (the next character input; if none, the last key press of the assignment). Windows with no estimate row at or before w or at or before w − 30 s. The number of excluded windows (inside long pauses) is reported.
- Statistic: the AUC pooling all positive × negative pairs within writers (ε = 0.001). The tie proportion is also reported. The degeneracy rule is not applied to H2 (on seen writers, the tie proportion in the unmatched comparison is below 5% for both signals).
- Re-matching (main): For each positive, draw up to 5 without replacement from the same writer's negatives that have the same decile of elapsed time within the block, and count positive × drawn negative pairs (duplicates counted once) within writer × decile. Reported alongside: a version that, in addition to the decile, also matches the quintile of the number of character key presses in [w − 30 s, w] (not used for the decision, because it may match away the run-up change itself).
- Attenuation R after re-matching = (unmatched AUC − re-matched AUC) / (unmatched AUC − 0.5). Re-matching changes not only the phase but also the pairs compared and the writers' weights, so R is "how much the AUC falls after the specified re-matching", not the proportion explained by phase itself. Report the numbers of unmatched pairs and re-matched pairs, and the number of positives for which no negative could be drawn. The interval of R is the percentile of a writer resampling (unmatched and re-matched on the same resample). If non-numeric iterations exceed 5%, R is undecidable (branch 3).
- Decision (for each signal):
  1. Lower bound of the unmatched AUC > 0.55 and lower bound of R ≥ 0.5 → supported. The wording is "Half or more of the rise in the unmatched comparison disappears when re-matched by work phase."
  2. Lower bound of the unmatched AUC > 0.55 and upper bound of R < 0.5 → "With the specified re-matching, it falls by less than half" (whether the re-matched AUC exceeds the band is not checked).
  3. Lower bound of the unmatched AUC > 0.55, and the interval of R straddles 0.5 or R is undecidable → undecidable (not supported even if the point estimate of R is 0.5 or more).
  4. Otherwise → write only that it was not replicated (no cause is stated).
- H2 is supported when both of the 2 signals are 1. The proportion of stalls that had a positive is reported.

## P8 Learners (H3)

- Logistic regression (scikit-learn; median imputation and missingness indicators within the training split; standardization; L2; C = 1.0; solver lbfgs; max_iter 1000) and gradient boosting (HistGradientBoostingClassifier; random_state = SEED + 2; early_stopping = False; otherwise defaults; missing values left as they are).
- Rows: the same stalls as in H1's main stratum (1; weight 1) and the controls drawn for those stalls (0; weight 1 / number drawn). In training, control rows are held per stall.
- Evaluation: Split the writers into 5 (GroupKFold; shuffle; random_state = SEED + 2), train on 4 and predict on 1. With the predicted probabilities, compute the P6 statistic (ε is not used; the same control is counted once).
- Interval: Each time the writers are resampled with replacement, the splitting and training are redone (200 iterations). Copies are grouped by the original writer into the same split, and pairs are built within copies. The interval is the percentile of the successful iterations. If training failures (iterations in which fit or predict raised a ValueError in any of the 5 folds; non-convergence warnings are not counted) exceed 5% (10 iterations), that learner is "undecidable". The branches and the precision guard are the same as in P6.
- Gate (recovery of a known truth): For one quantity of the same rows (the difference in character key presses per minute), create provisional data by adding a constant to the stall rows only so that that quantity's AUC alone (the P6 statistic; no ε) is 0.58, and check, with the same learner, splits, and interval (200 iterations), whether the lower bound of the interval exceeds 0.55. If it does not, or if training failures exceed 5%, that learner's H3 is "undecidable". Because this quantity takes only even integers, first add uniform jitter in [0, 1) to all rows (one per writer number × row id; both stall and control rows; SEED + 9) to break ties, and then add the constant. Report the AUC reached; if its difference from 0.58 exceeds 0.002, the gate does not pass. The added constant is not written. This gate checks that the learner can pick up a difference of known size on these rows and quantities; it does not guarantee that it can pick up actual run-up changes.
- Unmatched-window control (descriptive): Apply the same learners and quantities to the P7 windows (the quantities are built at w; up to 50 positives per writer, and 5 negatives per positive, at random; the negative weight is the value that makes the sums of positive and negative weights equal within the writer). Report the AUC and interval, but do not use them for the decision (on seen writers, neither of the 2 learners exceeded a lower bound of 0.55; Appendix C).
- H3 is descriptive. ### A1 Start of a stall (D3 and D4 of ADR-030, GSE's design-decision record; `src-tauri/prereg/p1-episode.json` 1.2.0; `docs/preregistration-p1-episode.md` §6.1 and §7.1)

- Kinds of key press: insertion kind = letters, digits, symbols, sentence punctuation (`.` `,`), space, newline, Tab. Deletion kind = Backspace, Delete. Others (modifiers, navigation, other) are not counted as production.
- Condition A: in the window (t − 30 s, t] at evaluation time t, the number of insertion-kind key presses − the number of deletion-kind key presses is less than 1. The set of evaluation times is {key press times} ∪ {key press times + 30 s} ∪ {s + θ}.
- s: the time at which condition A rose from false to true (not the time of the last key press; it is up to 30 s later). t0 = s + θ(s). It is an onset only when condition A stays true from s to t0 and t0 lies in the same observation segment. One onset per rise.
- θ(t): collect all intervals between two adjacent key presses (of any kind) that lie in the same observation segment, for the part at or before t, within the assignment (CS1) or the session (KLiCKe) (accumulating across observation segments; gaps between segments are not included as intervals); θ is their 0.99 quantile (nearest-rank: the ⌈0.99 n⌉-th in ascending order). With fewer than 100 intervals it is undefined, and no onset is raised.
- Observation segment: cut where the interval between the times of 1 Hz estimate rows exceeds 2 s. Before that, trimming is applied: take as end points h the rows whose difference from the next row is 60 s or more (just before a long gap) and the last row of the file; with R the row of the second that contains the last input (key press or key release) at or before h, drop the rows after max(R, h − 30 min) and at or before h (§6.1). Then, for segments where the difference between adjacent inputs (and the first and last rows of the file) is 30 min or more, drop the rows, keeping the row of the second that contains the earlier input and the row of the second that contains the later input (§7.1). The dropped rows are the union of the two rules.
- Onsets and θ are output by gse-replay `--features`, and the analysis reproduces the same rules in Python and checks that all cases agree (P11).
