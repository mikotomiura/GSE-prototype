Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "What moves with the pause definition and the adjustment: a multiverse
diagnostic of pause and stall labels in keystroke logs", for publication as a research article in the Journal of
Educational Data Mining.

Pause and stall rates derived from keystroke logs are used in educational data mining to relate writing and
programming behaviour to outcomes and to compare cohorts, yet each study fixes one definition. We applied a stall
detector and a fixed-threshold pause detector to four datasets released for research (two editions of an introductory
programming course and two writing corpora), varied the choices every such detector makes, and attached
writer-resampled intervals to each conclusion. The association between the label rate and a holistic essay score kept
its presence but changed direction at some levels, adjusting for essay length changed the reading as a definition
does, and comparisons between datasets moved, while the largest component of stall length stayed. From this map we
derive a reporting convention for pause and stall rates. We believe the manuscript fits the journal's readership
because it addresses how a common family of process measures should be reported before they are used in models or
interventions.

Related work by the author. A companion manuscript by the same author, "The 30-second Signal Changes of a Hand-set
Keystroke State Estimator before Code-writing Stalls Stayed inside a Preregistered Equivalence Band against Short
Pauses", is under review at this journal (submission 1484; preprint
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24026330). The two share the baseline stall definition, the conversion of one dataset, the
observation intervals and two opened holdouts, so a few baseline values can coincide; the questions, designs and
results differ, and Supplement S8 sets them side by side. Neither manuscript is anonymised.

results are archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24047695.
