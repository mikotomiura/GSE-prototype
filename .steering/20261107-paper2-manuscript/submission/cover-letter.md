To the Editor, Journal of Educational Data Mining

Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript "What moves with the pause definition and the adjustment: a multiverse diagnostic of pause
and stall labels in keystroke logs" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Educational Data Mining.

Pause and stall rates derived from keystroke logs are used in educational data mining to relate writing and
programming behaviour to outcomes and to compare cohorts, yet each study fixes one definition. The manuscript applies
a stall detector and a fixed-threshold pause detector to four datasets released for research (two editions of an
introductory programming course and two writing corpora), varies the choices every such detector makes, and attaches
writer-resampled intervals to each conclusion. The association between the label rate and a holistic essay score kept
its presence but changed direction at some levels, adjusting for essay length changed the reading as a definition
does, and comparisons between datasets moved, while the largest component of stall length stayed. From this map the
manuscript derives a reporting convention for pause and stall rates, which I believe fits the journal's readership
because it concerns how a common family of process measures should be reported before they are used in models or
interventions.

Related manuscript. A companion manuscript by the same author, "The 30-second signal changes of a hand-set keystroke
state estimator before code-writing stalls stayed inside a preregistered equivalence band against short pauses", is
under review at this journal (submission 1484; preprint https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24026330). The two share the
baseline stall definition, the conversion of one dataset, the observation intervals and two opened holdouts, so a few
baseline values can coincide; the questions, designs and results differ, and Supplement S8 sets them side by side.

Status of the analyses. Every computation ran under conditions committed to the author's version control before it
ran; these are not third-party registrations. Supplement S1 lists their order and marks which conclusions rest on
criteria set in advance and which were described after seeing the results. The conditions, the code and the aggregate
results are archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24047695.

Data. No new data were collected. The CS1 files were obtained from their authors on request, and I agreed to publish
only aggregate values. The essay (KLiCKe) and co-writing (CoAuthor) data are public and used under their terms.
