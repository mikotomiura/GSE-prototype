## Metadata

**[GSRR-1] Title.** TRACE: How studies of "AI fatigue" tie fatigue to AI, measure it and report it. Protocol for a registered methodological review.

TRACE is the name of this review's coding scheme (GSRR-43); it is not an acronym and not the name of a measure of fatigue. It links three things in each study: how fatigue is tied to AI (C1), what the fatigue items ask about (C2), and what the report states and concludes (C3 to C7: what is measured on the AI side, whether reporting is complete and internally consistent, whether samples overlap, and whether the title and the main conclusion pass three checks against the design, the level of attribution and the name of the main instrument). These checks compare designs, attribution levels and names; they are not validity evidence.

## Type, stages and dates

**[GSRR-5] Type of review.** Methodological review (meta-research). The main unit of analysis is the study report. Instrument-level codes (C2) use the instrument, and item-level codes (C2b) use the item (GSRR-43). We tabulate how studies attribute fatigue to AI, measure it and report it. ## Background and questions

**[GSRR-10] Background.**

*Rationale.* Work on "AI fatigue" is growing (Ragolane & Patel, 2025). One review calls for standardized, validated measures of AI fatigue (Sosiński & Piasecka, 2026); another places AI fatigue within digital burnout, with which it shares symptoms (Ragolane & Patel, 2025). Measurement research in general warns that a measure's name does not show what it measures, and that items are dropped, added and altered under the same name (Elson et al., 2023). Systematic reviews of AI-related self-report instruments are evaluating measurement properties with COSMIN (for example, Lintner, 2024). One registered review covers AI anxiety and attitude scales (Jonaid, 2026). The item-content coding in this review (C2b) is a researcher classification of item wording. It is not a COSMIN content-validity assessment, which requires experts and the target population. Studies of measurement practice in user-experience research have counted item disclosure, adaptation and reasons for choosing scales (Perrig et al., 2024). We did not find a review of AI-fatigue studies that tabulates, study by study, four things:
- how fatigue is tied to AI,
- what fatigue items ask about,
- whether reports are internally consistent, and
- whether titles and conclusions stay within the level of attribution, the design and the name of the instrument.

The third point was checked in 16 searches of free sources (Europe PMC, OpenAlex and a web search engine; 2026-10-17). The others were checked only in the reviews we had already read.

*Prior work by the same authors.* Our critical narrative review (unpublished draft, 2026; both authors of this review are its authors) described such problems qualitatively. It covered 11 reports drawn from a 34-record exploratory bibliography: the 10 primary studies in our exploration set and one report whose full text was unavailable. It was not preregistered.

*What this review does.* It describes, under rules fixed in advance (amendments after calibration are registered before main coding, GSRR-6), how studies of AI-related fatigue tie fatigue to AI, what their fatigue items ask about, and whether what their reports state and conclude stays within what they measured. For the problem types of GSRR-57 it reports whether each is present in the main set; these state whether a problem type is present or absent in the set, not how often it occurs, and they do not test whether the observations of the narrative review are reproduced. The observations of the narrative review are set beside them; the words "confirm", "replicate" and "replication" are not used about them. The primary counts come from records that were not used to build the rules. Their eligibility is decided after registration (GSRR-33).

*How the issues noted during exploration map to the codes.* The table maps the seven issues noted during exploration (below) to the codes. "Problem type" means the code has values that GSRR-57 reports as observed or not observed. The other codes are described.

**[GSRR-11] Primary research questions.** All primary answers come from the main set. They are descriptive (GSRR-57).
- **RQ1 (attribution).** At which level do studies tie their main fatigue instrument to AI? (C1)
- **RQ2 (instruments and items).** How do fatigue instruments break down by:
  - origin and modification (C2a)?
  - reported adaptation procedures (C2c)?
  - reported measurement properties (C2d)?
  - what their items ask about — fatigue, avoidance, irritation, futility, reduced interest (C2b)? For reduced interest, avoidance and futility, toward what target?
  - the type of instrument used for the AI-side variable (C3)?
- **RQ3 (reporting).** How complete and how internally consistent is reporting? (C4)
- **RQ4 (sample overlap).** Consider the records, the records linked as the same sample (E5, GSRR-35), and the records with signs of overlap that could not be confirmed. How many are there of each? How many studies remain when linked records are counted once? (C5) We do not establish independence.
- **RQ5 (title versus content).** Do titles and abstracts match the name of the main instrument, the design and the level of attribution? (C6)

**[GSRR-12] Secondary research questions.**
- **SQ1.** How are the answers to RQ2–RQ5 and SQ2 distributed within each of the two centre groups, centre-A and centre-B, assigned at screening (GSRR-33)? The tables of the two groups stand side by side; no statement says that a value is larger, smaller, more or less common in one group than in the other.
  - Centre-A: fatigue attributed to AI in its definition, measurement or analysis.
  - Centre-B: a general fatigue construct examined in relation to AI use.
  - RQ1 is not tabulated by these groups, because the grouping is defined by attribution and the tables would be circular. For the same reason, SQ1 leaves out C6c and reports C7 conditions (i) and (iii) only.
- **SQ2.** For each of the three conditions of C7, does the study's main conclusion meet it?

## Screening (eligibility)

**[GSRR-33] Screening stages.** For the 34 exploratory records, the source database's two screeners (authors 1 and 2) screened independently and reconciled their disagreements; this review takes those final decisions. The additional candidates are screened after registration by two screeners who judge each record independently within a stage (GSRR-34): author 1 and an AI screener (Claude Opus, Anthropic). There are three stages: title and abstract; full text; and centre (for records included at full text; the final value is centre-A or centre-B, and a screener may answer boundary).
- **Each stage.** Author 1 and the AI screener judge every record independently, with the same questions and decision rules (GSRR-36). The work goes in batches of nine records, cut in turn from the fixed screening order (only the last batch of a stage, and a batch cut short by 2026-12-31, may be smaller); at the full-text stage, the records that passed the title and abstract stage are batched in the same order. The centre stage is judged with the full-text stage, in the same batch: before the batch is committed, author 1 gives a centre to each record author 1 includes at full text, and the AI screener, in the same call, gives a centre to each record it includes at full text (attachment 6). For each batch and stage, author 1's judgements are committed to a version-controlled repository and pushed to its remote, with their time, before the AI screener is run on that batch; the AI screener writes its output to a file, whose format is checked by a script (attachment 6) that validates the JSON without displaying its content, and its SHA-256 and time are recorded. Both times are released (GSRR-41); they are recorded by the authors' own tools, not by a third party (GSRR-65). At the title and abstract stage, author 1 also judges from the title and abstract alone; the full texts are kept in a separate part of the screening database that author 1 opens only after committing the stage-1 judgements of the batch. Where the two differ, author 1 decides at that stage, after reading the AI screener's answers and the passages it cites (GSRR-38). Records that both screeners exclude at a stage are excluded; if their exclusion codes differ, author 1 decides the code. Records that both include go on to the next stage. At the full-text stage, a record that is pending for either screener, or that both leave pending, is decided by author 1 as include, exclude or pending; a record without a free full text stays pending with E6. A pending record whose free full text is found later is screened again at the full-text stage, in a batch of such records in the screening order, by the same procedure; every round is released, and the screening agreement counts each record once, at its latest round. A record still pending on 2026-12-31 is outside the main set and is counted in Table 3 and the flow diagram; screening continues after that date, and a record included before stage 5 begins goes to the late stratum (GSRR-39). At the centre stage either screener may answer boundary, but the final value is centre-A or centre-B, decided by author 1; no default rule is used. A record included at full text in the end may have no centre from the screener that did not include it. If that screener is author 1, author 1 gives the centre after the full-text decision, having read the AI screener's full-text answers but not its centre. If it is the AI screener, author 1 decides the centre alone, and the number of such records is reported; agreement at the centre stage is computed on the records with a centre from both screeners. The number of records that either screener judged boundary is reported, and for each such record its ID, which screener judged it boundary and the centre assigned are released (GSRR-41).
- **Same sample (E5).** At the full-text stage, a record is linked to another as the same sample only if its full text says so. The AI screener's answer to Q7 of attachment 6 (and an exclusion with E5) is treated as a signal of such a statement; author 1 identifies the other record and decides the link. A link is made only when the other record is among the additional candidates and is included at the full-text stage; the record with the earlier publication date (if equal, the first in the screening order) is coded, and the other is excluded with E5. If the other record is not among the candidates, is excluded or pending, or belongs to the exploration set, no E5 link is made: the record is coded, and its C5 value is "signs present but not confirmed". So every E5 group has exactly one coded record (GSRR-35). For the exploration set, the source database's links are used.
- **The AI screener.** It follows fixed instructions (attachment 6). They turn the source database's criteria (GSRR-24, GSRR-35) into seven questions, each answered `yes`, `no` or `unclear` with the place in the input that supports the answer, and fixed rules that turn the answers into a decision and an exclusion code. At the title and abstract stage, a record is included when in doubt. It judges the records of a batch in one call, each against the instructions; a batch is re-run at most once, and only when the script finds that the output is not in the required format; if the second output is also not in that format, the records of that batch are decided by author 1's judgements alone, and the number of re-runs and of such batches is reported. The wording that wraps a batch for the call and the settings of the call are fixed and attached (attachment 6, **[at registration]**). It sees only the instructions and the record's inputs from the screening database: the title and abstract (first stage; if no abstract was retrieved, the title alone) or the full text (later stages). It does not open web pages or use any other source, and it does not see the source database's classification, author 1's judgements or any earlier AI appraisal. One model identifier is used throughout (`claude-opus-5-5`, run as described in attachment 6); if it becomes unavailable, its successor is first checked on the 34 exploratory records against the pass lines of GSRR-37, and the change is reported as a deviation; if the successor misses a pass line, the AI screener's judgements become advisory only and author 1 decides every remaining record, as GSRR-37 provides.
- **Full texts.** Both screeners use the same full text: only versions that are publicly and freely available. They are retrieved by script (open-access locations listed by OpenAlex, Europe PMC and Semantic Scholar), then by author 1 in a web browser; author 1 passes any bot check and saves PDFs that the browser only displays. A full text from Europe PMC may be its full-text XML, which is the publisher's text extracted by machine. No full text is retyped or rewritten. If no free full text is found, the record is pending with E6 (GSRR-35).
- **After registration**, author 1 knows these coding rules. To limit the effect of that knowledge:
  - the remaining candidates are all 264 additional candidates, including the 79 that author 1 screened before registration (GSRR-32) and the 43 review-search records; the earlier judgements are not shown to author 1 during screening;
  - they are screened in a fixed order: within each population group the record IDs, sorted alphabetically, are shuffled with Python `random.shuffle`, using a generator freshly seeded with 20261018 for each group; the groups are then interleaved, one record from each group in the order listed in GSRR-16, so that progress by 2026-12-31 does not depend on the group;
  - records with no population group in the source database are placed in the group unknown. Before registration, 51 of the 264 additional candidates had none: the 43 review-search records, seven records that the source database's machine classification labels review-type, and one record added by hand. To see why, an AI assistant read their titles and abstracts twice, in two independent readings with fixed definitions, only for document type and population group, not for eligibility: both readings found 50 to be documents without participants (reviews, study or review protocols, commentaries and a correction notice); the readings differed on the remaining record, which has no abstract, and its document type was left undetermined. No human read the abstracts of the 43 review-search records for this purpose;
  - the resulting order list and its SHA-256 are attached to the registration, with the Python version used **[at registration]**;
  - no additional candidate is coded during screening (calibration on the exploration set runs in parallel, GSRR-44);
  - every screening decision made after registration is released with its date, author 1's judgement, the AI screener's judgement and the exclusion code (GSRR-41);
  - exclusions for E2 and E3 are counted by population group, and the candidates still unscreened on 2026-12-31 are counted by population group.

**[GSRR-34] Screened fields / blinding.** Author 1 screens from a database that contains the bibliographic data, the abstract and the free full text of each record and no judgement: neither the AI screener's, nor author 1's earlier ones, nor the AI appraisals author 1 read before 2026-10-17. Before registration, author 1 had screened 79 additional candidates and read AI-generated preliminary appraisals before judging (GSRR-32); author 1 may remember them (GSRR-65). The AI screener is blind to all judgements (GSRR-33). Blinding holds within a stage only: for a record on which the two screeners differed, author 1 has read the AI screener's answers before judging that record at the next stage.

**[GSRR-35] Used exclusion criteria.**
- E1: AI only as a fatigue-detection tool.
- E2: fatigue not measured.
- E3: no relation to AI use.
- E4: not a primary study.
- E5: duplicate report or secondary report of the same sample.
- E6: full text unavailable.
- E7: language other than English or Japanese.

E5 records are not coded. They stay linked to their group for C5 (GSRR-43). Each E5 group has exactly one coded record (GSRR-33), whose C5 value is "linked as the same sample"; no other coded record has that value. An uncoded E5 record is counted in the RQ4 record counts with the set and population group of its group's coded record.

**[GSRR-36] Screener instructions.** Both screeners use the questions of attachment 6 (sections 1 to 4) and its decision rules: inclusion when in doubt at the title and abstract stage, the order of exclusion codes, and the definitions of pending and of the centre values. Author 1 records a decision and a code for each record and does not fill in the AI screener's output format. The AI screener receives the whole of attachment 6, with its SHA-256.

**[GSRR-37] Screening reliability.**
- *Exploratory records.* The source database's two screeners agreed on 32 of the 34 exploratory records on three categories. That is exploration material.
- *AI screener, checked before registration.* The pass lines were set before the AI screener was applied: at the title and abstract stage, none of the 13 records that the two human screeners finally included or left pending is excluded; at the full-text stage and the centre stage, Cohen's kappa of at least 0.6 against the human final decisions. If a line was missed, the instructions could be revised once and applied again; if it was still missed, the AI screener's judgements would be advisory only and author 1 would decide every record. The instructions were written from the source database's criteria without looking at the human decisions, but those criteria were themselves refined on these 34 records, so the accuracy may be optimistic. Round 1 met all three lines (title and abstract: 13 of 13 passed on, 19 of 21 exclusions also excluded; full text: 33 of 34 agreed, kappa 0.945; centre: 10 of 10 agreed). One question (whether fatigue is measured) was then clarified for the title and abstract stage, because the AI screener had excluded records whose abstracts listed measures without fatigue. Round 2 also met all three lines (title and abstract: 13 of 13 passed on, 16 of 21 exclusions also excluded; full text: 32 of 34 agreed, kappa 0.893; centre: 10 of 10 agreed). In rounds 1 and 2 the instructions allowed the AI screener to open a record's web page when no abstract had been retrieved; this was then removed, so that both screeners see the same inputs. The pre-set rule called for neither revision, because every line had been met: both are departures from the rule and are reported as such. Because the instructions were revised twice and applied three times to the same records, the accuracy may be further optimistic. Round 3 applied the registered instructions, without web access, to the same records in batches of at most nine (title and abstract: 13 of 13 passed on, 16 of 21 exclusions also excluded, 4 records judged from the title alone; full text: 32 of 34 agreed, kappa 0.893; centre: 10 of 10 agreed, centre-A 7 and centre-B 3, kappa 1.0). Round 3 gave the same decisions as round 2. The pass lines were committed before round 1, and the instructions before each round; the commits and the record-by-record judgements of all three rounds are given with attachment 6, with the two records on which round 3 differed from the human decision.
- *Additional candidates.* For each stage, the agreement between author 1 and the AI screener before reconciliation is reported as percentage agreement and Cohen's kappa, with the number of records each screener sent on, the number of differences decided as author 1 had judged and as the AI screener had judged, and, at the centre stage, the number of records judged boundary. Because author 1 decides every difference, this agreement describes how often the two screeners agreed; it is not the AI screener's accuracy. A drop in agreement below the pass lines does not change the procedure; it is reported. Agreement is also reported separately for the first and second halves of the screening order, because author 1 reads the AI screener's answers on differing records as screening proceeds.

**[GSRR-38] Screening reconciliation procedure.** Author 1 decides every record on which author 1 and the AI screener differ, at the stage where they differ, after reading the AI screener's answers and cited passages. Both judgements before reconciliation are kept and released (GSRR-41), with the number of differences decided each way (GSRR-37). The main set therefore rests on one human screener and an AI screener, not on two human screeners; sensitivity analysis 5 (GSRR-62) repeats the full-text and centre screening with two human screeners after the manuscript draft, if author 2 can do it.

**[GSRR-39] Sampling and sample size.**
- **Exploration set.** The 10 primary studies the source database included from the 34-record bibliography, and the four item-coded instruments. Used for calibration and coded again after calibration (GSRR-6). Its tables are flagged as calibration material.
- **Main set.** Primary studies among the additional candidates whose final decision after reconciliation (GSRR-38) is to include, recorded on or before 2026-12-31. Excluded from it:
  - the exploration records, even if found again;
  - records without a final full-text decision on that date.
- **Late stratum.** Studies included after 2026-12-31 and before stage 5 begins are reported as a separate stratum, outside the primary counts. They are coded twice by author 1 under the same rules. Studies included after stage 5 begins are listed but not coded.
- **Calibration.** Calibration uses all primary studies of the exploration set (GSRR-44). No record of the main set is used for calibration or removed from it.
- **Minimum size.** The minimum is counted in coded records, not instruments; E5-linked records are not coded (GSRR-35), so each coded record is one study. It applies to every table on its own: a table (a set, a population group or a centre) based on fewer than 10 studies reports counts only, without proportions. The 10-study rule does not apply to the proportions of item types within one instrument, which follow the C2b rule. For C2b, if fewer than 3 instruments with available items are first seen in the main set, each instrument's counts are still given, without proportions. The number of "items unavailable" instruments is always shown next to the C2b tables.

**[GSRR-40] Screening procedure justification.** The source database had a second human screener only for the 34 exploratory records. A second human screening of the 264 additional candidates before coding would fall on the co-author, whose time is limited during the screening period. An AI screener, checked against the human decisions on the 34 records before registration, is used as the independent second screener instead. The two-human screening is moved to after the manuscript draft, when the co-author reads the draft anyway, and is limited to the full-text and centre stages of the records that either screener sent to full text; it is reported as a sensitivity analysis (GSRR-62). If it has not begun within 8 weeks of the draft being sent to the co-author, it is not done (GSRR-62).

**[GSRR-41] Data management and sharing.** The list of record IDs, with author 1's judgement and the AI screener's judgement at each stage, whether either screener judged the record boundary at the centre stage, the E5 links, the final decision, the exclusion code and the decision date, will be released with the code table (GSRR-50). The AI screener's outputs are released with their quotations, which are limited to 25 words each, except quotations of scale items, which are removed before release; no abstract or full text is released. Before release, author 1 marks every quotation and note of the AI screener as a scale item or not (a scale item reproduces, in whole or in part, the wording of an item of a questionnaire or scale); longer descriptions of the place cited (more than 8 words, or with quotation marks) are checked in the same way; a script pre-marks likely items to help, removes the marked ones, and checks that none of them is left in the public files (attachment 6). For the 25-word limit, a word is a whitespace-separated token with a letter or digit, and two Japanese characters count as one word. The times of author 1's commits and of the AI screener's outputs are released (GSRR-33).

**[GSRR-42] Miscellaneous screening details.** On 2026-10-17, the screening of the additional candidates had stopped part-way through a list ordered by population group. Some groups, such as health professionals, had no human screening yet. All 264 records are screened again in the fixed order (GSRR-33), and every result table shows counts by population group.

## Extraction (coding)

**[GSRR-43] Entities to extract.**

General rules:
- A missing code ("not reported", "not verifiable", "not applicable", "unresolved") stands alone in a cell; it is never combined with another value in a multi-value cell.
- Missing values have three codes. "Not reported" means absent from the report. "Not verifiable" means we could not access the needed text. "Not applicable" means the item does not apply.
- **Units.**
  - C1 and C3–C7 are coded per record.
  - C2 is coded per fatigue instrument within a record.
  - C2b is coded per item.
- **Fatigue instrument.** C1, C2 and C6a refer to the study's fatigue instrument(s), not to instruments for the AI-side variable.
- **Set of an instrument version.** A version belongs to the first set, in the order exploration, main, late, in which a record uses it. Its items take the same set. "First seen in the main set" means this set is the main set.
- **Instrument.** The set of items actually administered in the study, for example a subscale only when only the subscale was used. An instrument version is identified by its item wording together with its language. Items are coded as printed in the report, including translations, and the language is recorded. If the report gives no items but states that an existing scale was used without modification, the items are taken from the cited source and flagged (`item_source` = source). For such an instrument, C2a origin is the existing scale's name or "existing general scale, wording not verifiable", and each kind of modification is "not verifiable", because a comparison with the source would compare the source with itself. Otherwise the instrument is coded "items unavailable". The C2b denominator is the number of coded items: the printed items, or the items taken from the cited source. The instrument's full item count is shown next to it.
- **Main instrument.** The fatigue instrument whose variable is named first in the abstract's results. If the abstract has no results, it is the instrument described first in the methods.
- **Main relation.** The first relation between an AI-side variable and fatigue reported in the abstract's results.
- **Abstract's results.** The sentences of the abstract that report a finding, with or without a heading.

**C4f recalculation rules.**
- **What is recalculated.**
  - p-values from reported test statistics and degrees of freedom (t, F, r, χ², z). A z is recalculated only when the report gives it, including a column labelled z or critical ratio in a table of estimates; the coder does not form z from an estimate and its standard error. Tests are two-tailed unless the report says otherwise. A p-value that the report says is adjusted for multiple testing is not counted. A p-value reported as an inequality (for example, p < .001) is consistent if the recalculated value satisfies it. A p-value printed as p = .000 is read as p < .001, and "ns" or "n.s." as p ≥ .05 (or p ≥ the α the report states). A test statistic reported with neither a p-value nor a significance marker is not recalculated. An inconsistency that changes the conclusion at α = .05 is also counted separately as a gross inconsistency.
  - Values in tables are included. Degrees of freedom given in a table note are used; degrees of freedom that would have to be inferred from the sample size are not, and the result is "cannot be calculated". A p-value shown only by asterisks is treated as an inequality at the threshold stated in the table note; without a stated threshold it is "cannot be calculated". "Cannot be calculated" stays in the denominator (GSRR-53). A test reported in both the text and a table is counted once, from the text. Each result records whether it came from the text or a table.
  - Percentages from counts and denominators.
  - An indirect effect as a×b, only for a single-mediator path where a, b and the indirect effect are reported at the same level of standardization.
- **Who recalculates.** Author 1 recalculates in each of the two coding passes, independently of the other pass. The AI draft is generated by author 1 and stored unopened, with its SHA-256 recorded. It is opened only after the values of both passes and of the third pass (GSRR-48) are fixed, and is then used only as a cross-check; its agreement with the human values is reported. Data are the reconciled human values.
- These rules follow the logic of statcheck (Nuijten et al., 2016), which recalculates p from t, F, r, χ² and Z with their degrees of freedom, with four differences: the coder judges each test rather than scanning the whole text, so a test is one-tailed only if the report says one-tailed, one-sided or directional for that test; values printed in tables are included; p-values that the report says are adjusted for multiple testing are not counted; and percentages and indirect effects are added. The procedure is carried out by people, not by statcheck.
- **Tolerance.** A value is consistent if it falls within the range implied by rounding the inputs to their reported decimals.
- **Unit.** Each recalculated result. Each record is then summarised by the first value that applies: any inconsistency (at least one inconsistent result); none (at least one consistent result and no inconsistent one); cannot be calculated (results to recalculate, none of which could be calculated); not applicable (no result to recalculate). The gross-inconsistency summary uses the same order: any gross inconsistency (at least one grossly inconsistent result); none (at least one calculated result and no grossly inconsistent one); cannot be calculated; not applicable. Because results cluster in records, the number of recalculated results per record and the number of consistent, inconsistent and grossly inconsistent results are also reported (GSRR-56).

**C6b causal wording rules.**
- **Causal terms** (in the title or abstract, counted only in a claim about a relation between an AI-side variable and fatigue, and in inflected forms of the same word only):
  - effect of / effect on, impact of / impact on, influence of / influence on, affect (verb only), cause, lead to, result in, induce, drive (verb only), trigger, due to, because of, consequence;
  - increase or reduce, when the AI-side variable is the grammatical subject (for example, "AI use increases fatigue");
  - the Japanese 影響, 効果, 引き起こす, もたらす, and 〜による when it states a cause (not a means, as in AIによる支援).
- **Not counted:**
  - statistical terms (direct, indirect or total effect; effect size; mediating, moderating or interaction effect);
  - compound modifiers and construct names (AI-induced, AI-driven, AI-related, AI-based, and the construct name "AI-induced fatigue");
  - affect as a noun and affective;
  - "associated with", "predict", "relationship", "correlate".
- A negated causal claim, such as "does not affect", is counted.
- **Design categories and manipulation.** "Manipulation" means the AI-side variable was manipulated, in every design:
  - no manipulation: cross-sectional; longitudinal or time-lagged; diary or experience sampling; case study; qualitative; scale development; other or unclear;
  - manipulation: randomized experiment, quasi-experiment or pre–post study in which the AI-side variable is the intervention;
  - a design with an intervention unrelated to AI counts as no manipulation; a mixed design counts as manipulation if any component manipulates the AI-side variable.
- **Mismatch:** causal wording in a study whose design has no manipulation.
- **Match:** any other design, or "no causal wording".

**[GSRR-44] Extraction stages.** Author 1 codes every record twice, at least four weeks apart, and the second pass is made without looking at the first. Author 2 codes part or all of the main set once, independently (step 6). Two kinds of agreement are therefore reported: between the two passes of author 1 (consistency of one coder), and between author 1 and author 2 on the records author 2 coded (agreement between coders). Independent coding is claimed only for the records author 2 coded.
1. **Worked examples.** Before calibration, author 1 tries the definitions on worked examples that include multiple instruments, a qualitative study and missing values.
2. **Calibration** (GSRR-39). Author 1 codes all primary studies of the exploration set twice: the first pass in November 2026 and the second in December 2026, at least four weeks later. Agreement between the two passes is computed on each of these units: each C1 level; C2a, C2c and C2d per instrument; C2b primary type, boundary and target per item; C3, C4 (a)–(d), each column of C4 (e), C5, C6a, C6b and the C6c title input per record; the C4f judgement of each result recalculated in both passes; the conclusion sentence, whether the conclusion and the main instrument could be identified, whether the conclusion uses an AI-fatigue term, and the construct-term family of the conclusion. For every unit with at least 5 comparisons (records, instruments, items or results, as the unit requires) and below 80% percentage agreement, the definitions are amended and the amendment is registered before main coding; units with fewer than 5 comparisons are reported but do not trigger an amendment, so they are not calibrated (GSRR-65). The units with fewer than 5 comparisons are listed with the calibration results. An amendment is based only on the disagreements between the two calibration passes, and the record IDs it rests on are listed with it; nothing read while screening the additional candidates is used. Amended definitions are not checked again before main coding; their agreement is seen in the two passes of main coding (GSRR-47) and noted as a limitation (GSRR-65). Derived values and the agreement with the AI draft are not criteria. Kappa is reported alongside percentage agreement but is not a criterion, because rare categories make it unstable in 10 studies. Calibration codes are not data.
3. **Main coding.** Author 1 codes all records of the main set, the late stratum and the exploration set twice under the final codebook, at least four weeks apart. The first pass takes the records in ascending order of record ID, and is saved with its SHA-256 and closed before the second begins; the second pass takes the same list in an order drawn with Python `random.shuffle`, using a generator freshly seeded with 20261020.
4. **Use of exploration codes for instruments.** If a main-set study uses an instrument already coded in the exploration set, its C2b values come from the exploration values of the main coding (under the final codebook, not the calibration codes), but only if the item wording is verbatim identical. Otherwise the instrument is coded as a new version.
5. **AI drafts** are allowed only for C4f and C5.
   - For C4f, author 1 recomputes every value in both passes before seeing the draft, and the draft is opened only after the third pass (GSRR-43, GSRR-48); the agreement between human and AI values is reported.
   - For C5, the AI bibliographic matches are generated by author 1 and stored unopened, with their SHA-256 recorded. In each pass, author 1 first records the signs of overlap from the reports; the matches are opened only after both passes and the third pass have fixed the C5 codes, and every match is then checked.

6. **Second coder.** Before stage 5 begins, author 2 states whether they will code all records of the main set or a random sample; this choice is made before any main-set record is coded, depends only on author 2's time, and is recorded with its date. The sample is at least 20% of the coded main-set studies and at least 5 (all if there are 5 or fewer): ceil(0.20 × n) records, drawn with Python `random.sample` from the record IDs sorted alphabetically, using a generator freshly seeded with 20261021 (script, GSRR-64). Before coding, author 2 reads the codebook and tries the worked examples of step 1; author 2 does not take part in calibration. Author 2 codes once, under the final codebook, without seeing author 1's codes, the AI drafts or the version-1 codebook values. This follows the practice of having a second reviewer code a sample when one reviewer codes the rest (Shea et al., 2017, item 6). If author 2 cannot begin within 8 weeks of the main set being closed, a person who did not write the rules may be asked to code the sample instead; if no one can, it is not done, and the review is reported as single-coder, as a deviation.

**[GSRR-45] Extractor instructions.** This protocol, the C2b codebook (version 2) and a coding form with the definitions above.

**[GSRR-46] Extractor blinding.** Author 1 knows the research questions and the rules. The second pass is made without looking at the first. The C4f AI draft and the C5 bibliographic matches are opened only after both passes and the third pass are fixed (GSRR-43, GSRR-44, GSRR-48). The version-1 codebook values are not consulted. Author 2 codes without seeing author 1's codes or the AI drafts (GSRR-44, step 6). Author 1 wrote the earlier narrative review, so the codes carry author 1's view of what counts as a problem (GSRR-65).

**[GSRR-47] Extraction reliability.** Agreement between author 1 (reconciled codes) and author 2 is reported on the records author 2 coded, for the same codes and units as below, with percentage agreement and kappa; it is set beside the threshold of good agreement in Shea et al. (2017), at least 80%, which is a point of reference, not a stopping rule. Agreement between the two passes of author 1:
- C1: Cohen's kappa for each level, as a binary code, and for the representative value.
- C2b: kappa per item for the primary type, for `boundary`, and for the secondary type among items marked as boundary in both passes.
- C2a, C2c and C2d: kappa per instrument.
- C2b target: kappa over all items, with "no target" as a category.
- C3, C4 (a)–(d), each column of C4 (e), C5, C6a, C6b, the C6c title input, and the C4f record summary and its gross-inconsistency summary: kappa per record.
- C4f per result: the number of results recalculated in only one pass, and, for results recalculated in both passes, kappa for the judgement (consistent / inconsistent / grossly inconsistent / cannot be calculated).
- C6c and C7 are derived, so no kappa is computed for them. For the conclusion sentence, whether the conclusion and the main instrument could be identified, whether the conclusion uses an AI-fatigue term, and the construct-term family of the conclusion, the percentage of identical choices is reported.
- C4f: agreement between the reconciled values and the AI draft, per recalculated result. It is reported only.
- Records coded in only one pass are counted and reported.
- Percentage agreement is reported alongside every kappa.
- 95% CIs by bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 20261017) over records, and over instrument versions for item-level codes.
- If both passes use a single category, kappa is undefined and only percentage agreement is reported.
- Reported separately for the calibration passes, the exploration set and the main set.

**[GSRR-48] Extraction reconciliation procedure.**
- Disagreements between the two passes are resolved by a third pass by author 1, made with both earlier passes and the full text visible.
- For the records author 2 coded, differences between author 1's reconciled codes and author 2's codes are then discussed by both, with the full text; the agreed value is the data for those records. Records author 2 did not code keep author 1's reconciled values.
- Disagreements that remain are coded "unresolved". Unresolved codes are excluded from proportions, and their number is reported; the exception is C2b, where unresolved items stay in the item denominator as their own category (GSRR-53). A derived value (the C1 representative value, the C4f record summary, C6c, C7) is "unresolved" if any of its inputs is.
- A sensitivity analysis uses each pass's values in turn (GSRR-62).
- Codes of both passes and the reconciled codes are kept.

**[GSRR-49] Extraction procedure justification.**
- Each code answers one question: RQ1 = C1; RQ2 = C2a–C2d and C3; RQ3 = C4; RQ4 = C5; RQ5 = C6; SQ2 = C7. SQ1 is answered by grouping.
- Most codes require judgement. Coding every record by two people would fall on the co-author, whose time is limited, so author 1 codes every record twice and author 2 codes a random sample of at least 20%, or all records if author 2 can (Shea et al., 2017, item 6). The two passes show how consistently one coder applies the rules; the agreement with author 2 shows, on the records author 2 coded, whether another person applies them in the same way.

**[GSRR-50] Data management and sharing.**
- At publication, the following go in the same OSF project as this registration: the code table (CSV), the codebook and the coding form under CC BY 4.0, and the scripts under the MIT licence.
- The public code table gives codes, item numbers and source pages. It does not reproduce scale items verbatim.
- A per-instrument summary table (each instrument version, its origin and modifications, and its C2b type composition) is released with the code table.
- The codebook quotes short phrases from items as anchors, with their sources.

**[GSRR-51] Miscellaneous extraction details.** Notes and version-1 codes made during exploration are not data. Only codes made under this protocol are data.

  - **Instruments used**: every instrument version used by a study of the set, whichever set it was first seen in, with its language, the number of studies using it, its format, whether its items refer to AI (C1 L1a, a property of the wording), the number of records using it with each value of the properties that can differ between records (instructions referring to AI, C1 L1b; origin; item disclosure), its number of items (printed / full), and its item counts by type (counts only). This table lists; it does not compare sets.
  - **C4f per result**: the counts per recalculated result, by source (text or table), and the number of results per record, beside the per-record summary.
  - **Per-study table** (supplement): one row per coded study with its C1–C7 values; no item text.
  - For C5 and RQ4, records and studies (E5-linked records counted once) are counted separately. E5 records are not coded otherwise, so C1–C4, C6 and C7 tables count coded records.
- All tables are shown separately for the main set, the exploration set and the late stratum. The record-level tables, C4f per result and the RQ4 counts are also shown by population group. The C2b tables and the instruments used are shown by set only, because one instrument version can be used by studies of several population groups.
- SQ1 is answered by tables split into centre-A and centre-B, set side by side without comparison statements.
- Descriptive only: no significance tests, no prevalence estimates and no comparisons between sets.
- If part of the plan cannot be done (GSRR-39 minimum sizes), we report what was not done and why.

**[GSRR-57] Criteria for conclusions.**
- **Problem types** are fixed as follows (C1 is described, not a problem type, because the eligibility criteria admit studies of a general fatigue construct examined in relation to AI use, GSRR-24; the problem is claiming "AI fatigue" on such a basis, which C6c captures in the title and C7 (ii) in the conclusion):
  - C4: "inconsistent" in rules (a)–(d); "any inconsistency" and, separately, "any gross inconsistency" in rule (f); in rule (e), "codebook not available" and "agreement: none";
  - C5: "signs present but not confirmed";
  - C6a: "different family"; C6b: "mismatch"; C6c: "AI-fatigue term with L3–L6";
  - C7: each condition separately, as three problem types: (i) causal wording not supported by the design; (ii) an AI-fatigue term in the conclusion while the main instrument's C1 representative value is L3 to L6; (iii) the construct term of the conclusion and the name of the main instrument in different term families. (iii) and C6a are name checks; they do not compare the content of the instruments.
- C2b types are not problem types. Their distribution is described, without calling any type a problem, because this review's eligibility definition includes motivational and behavioural depletion (GSRR-24).
- **Evaluable records.** A record is evaluable for a problem type only if its value can show or not show the problem: C4 (a)–(d) consistent or inconsistent; C4 (e) a codebook value of yes or no, and an agreement value of value given, index named only or none; C4f any inconsistency or none (records with at least one calculated result), and likewise for the gross summary; C5 any category; C6a same or different family; C6b match or mismatch; C6c any of its three derived values other than "cannot decide"; each condition of C7 met or not met. Other values ("not reported", "no criterion stated", "no comparison possible", "cannot be calculated", "cannot decide", "no term to compare", "instrument name has no family term", "instrument unnamed", "no construct term in the title") make a record not evaluable for that problem type.
- The main set is the set decided by author 1 and the AI screener (GSRR-33, GSRR-39). Whether each statement holds when the main set is decided by two human screeners is reported as sensitivity analysis 5 (GSRR-62).
- For each problem type, among the evaluable main-set records: if at least one shows it, we write that it "was observed in the main set"; if none does, that it "was not observed in the main set"; if no record is evaluable, that it "could not be evaluated". Each statement gives the count, the number of evaluable records, the number not evaluable and the number unresolved. In the abstract, the abstract rule of the outline applies (attachment 5).
- Values inherited from the exploration coding (GSRR-44, step 4) concern C2b, which is not a problem type, so they never enter a problem statement.
- The words "confirm", "replicate" and "replication" are not used about the observations of the narrative review, and no word of frequency or severity ("common", "rare", "widespread", "serious") is used about a problem type.
- We do not conclude that problems are more or less common than in the exploration set, or in one centre or population group than in another.
- C7 checks three conditions under which a conclusion goes beyond what was measured: the design, the level of attribution and the names. It does not judge whether a finding is true, and it does not compare the item content of an instrument with the conclusion.

**[GSRR-58] Synthesist blinding.** None. The synthesis is computed by script from the code table.

**[GSRR-59] Synthesis reliability.** All tables are produced by script from the code table, and every result value in the manuscript is copied from the script output by a script, not typed by hand. Author 1 checks every number in the manuscript against the script output. No second person checks the numbers (GSRR-65).

**[GSRR-60] Synthesis reconciliation procedure.** Discrepancies are traced to the code table and the script and corrected.

**[GSRR-61] Publication bias analyses.** Not applicable (no effect estimates).

**[GSRR-62] Sensitivity analyses.** Analyses 1, 2, 4 and 5 are applied to the problem statements of GSRR-57; analysis 3 is applied to the C2b tables. Analysis 5 is also applied to the record-level tables of the main set.
1. Peer-reviewed records only.
2. Records in the C5 category "signs present but not confirmed" removed.
3. C2b counted with secondary types included: each boundary item counts once for its primary type and once for its secondary type, and the denominator stays the number of coded items.
4. Each pass's values in place of the reconciled values.
5. **Main set decided by two human screeners.** After the manuscript draft is written, author 2 reads it, and then authors 1 and 2 screen again, each on their own and then together, at the full-text stage and the centre stage: every additional candidate that author 1 or the AI screener sent to the full-text stage (GSRR-33). Records sent to the full-text stage after 2026-12-31 are not included. Records that both excluded at the title and abstract stage are not screened again. Both screeners screen from the screening database without the judgements of the main screening; author 1 may remember them, so the independence this adds is mainly author 2's. Differences are settled by discussion; records still unsettled are counted as unresolved and kept out of the set of this analysis. Both screeners then know the results, so this screening is not blind to them; it shows whether the problem statements depend on how the main set was screened, not a screening free of that knowledge. Studies that this screening adds to the main set are coded once by author 1 under the final rules and flagged as single-pass; studies it removes are dropped. The tables and problem statements are computed again, and every difference from the main analysis is reported. Both screeners' judgements before reconciliation and the final decisions are released (GSRR-41). If any problem statement differs from the main analysis, the scope sentence of the abstract says so. If this screening has not begun within 8 weeks of the manuscript draft being sent to author 2, it is not done and no one else is asked; if it begins and stops before the end, the part done is reported as partial in Table 13, with the number of records screened. If it is not done: Table 13 shows the column empty with the reason, and Table 2, the limitations and the scope sentence of the abstract state that no two-human screening was done.

**[GSRR-63] Synthesis procedure justification.** The questions ask what practices are present in the coded set. Counts with denominators answer them; proportions describe the coded set and are not estimates of how often a practice occurs in the literature.

**[GSRR-64] Synthesis data management and sharing.** As in GSRR-50. The synthesis script `c_synthesis.py` implements:
- the screening order and the draw of the second coder's sample;
- the derived values (C1 representative value, C4f summary, C6c, C7 and each of its conditions);
- agreement per set and unit (GSRR-47), and the screening agreement between author 1 and the AI screener per stage, with the differences decided each way and the records judged boundary (GSRR-37);
- the tables of GSRR-56 by set; the record-level tables also by population group and by centre (the centre tables leave out C1, C6c and C7 (ii), GSRR-12); C4f per result and the RQ4 counts also by population group; the C2b tables and the instruments used by set only; including C2a, C2c, C2d, C2b per instrument, the instruments used, C4f per result, the per-study table and RQ4 study counts;
- the problem statements of GSRR-57;
- sensitivity analyses 1–4 of GSRR-62. Analysis 5 is computed by running the same script on the code tables of the main set as decided in that screening.

The script stops if a required file or column is missing, a cell is empty or a value of any coded column is outside its code list, including impossible combinations (a gross inconsistency in a result that is not inconsistent) and a repeated value in a multi-value code. `make_dummy.py` builds a dummy code table, runs the script on it and checks boundary cases with assertions: the screening order, C1, C6c and C7 derivation (two-family names, missing terms, unresolved inputs, "cannot decide" when only the main instrument is unknown, the centre tables without C1, C6c and C7 (ii), each condition of C7 on its own), denominators, evaluable records per problem type (including a set in which no record is evaluable), the C4f summary order (a record with consistent and incalculable results), two-pass agreement on C4f results, minimum sizes in studies, multi-value codes, version sets, C2b with and without secondary types, the instruments used (including versions first seen in another set, whether a version refers to AI, and versions whose records differ in instructions, origin or disclosure), C4f per result (including by source), the per-study table, agreement coverage, sensitivity outputs, input validation and E5 links. Both files are attached to the registration with their SHA-256 **[at registration]**.

**[GSRR-65] Miscellaneous synthesis details.**
- Amendments after registration are logged with their date, reason and affected items, and listed in the report.
- Known limitations:
  - The rules were built from the exploration set.
  - Before registration, author 1 had screened 79 additional candidates, after reading AI-generated preliminary appraisals (GSRR-34). Author 1 screens them again without these judgements, but may remember them.
  - The main set rests on one human screener and an AI screener, not on two human screeners. The AI screener's accuracy was checked on the 34 exploratory records, whose criteria were refined on those same records, and which include more studies of centre-A and a different mix of population groups than the additional candidates; its accuracy on the additional candidates may be lower (GSRR-37).
  - Only free full texts are used. Records without one stay pending (E6) and are not coded; this includes records whose abstract alone did not settle whether fatigue was measured.
  - The two-human screening (sensitivity analysis 5) is done after the results are known, and may not be done at all if author 2 cannot do it.
  - The AI screener judges up to nine records in one call, so the records of a batch may influence one another.
  - The AI screener's web access and reading of other files are forbidden by its instructions, not blocked technically (attachment 6).
  - Author 1 reads the AI screener's answers on differing records while screening goes on, so author 1's later judgements may move towards the AI screener's; agreement is reported for the first and second halves of the screening order (GSRR-37).
  - Author 1 records a decision and a code for each record, not the answer to each question, so whether author 1 applied each question cannot be checked from the record (GSRR-36).
  - The times of commits and outputs are recorded by the authors' own tools and pushed to a remote repository; they are not certified by a third party (GSRR-33).
  - Coding units with fewer than 5 comparisons in calibration are not calibrated (GSRR-44).
  - The AI screener's judgements are independent of author 1's, but its instructions were written by author 1 from criteria refined on the same 34 records; the independence is of judgement, not of perspective.
  - C6a and C7 (iii) compare names. "Within what was measured" does not mean that the item content supports the conclusion.
  - Before registration, human screening of the additional candidates was uneven across population groups. The fixed, interleaved order spreads screening across groups, and records still unscreened on 2026-12-31 are counted by group (GSRR-33).
  - The search drew on free sources only.
  - The eligibility criteria shape the outcomes. E3 removes studies with no relation to AI, so C1 "L6 none" is truncated. The fatigue definition includes motivational depletion, so the share of `MOT` items is partly set at entry.
  - Item content does not show what scores contain.
  - If author 2 codes a sample, most records are coded by one person in two passes, and agreement between coders is known only for the sample (GSRR-44). Both authors wrote the earlier narrative review, so agreement between them is agreement between coders who share that view (GSRR-46). Author 2 does not take part in calibration.
  - Calibration uses studies that the coder had read and partly coded while building the rules, so calibration agreement may be higher than agreement on new studies. Amended definitions are not checked before main coding (GSRR-44).
  - Calibration runs while the coder screens the additional candidates; amendments rest only on calibration disagreements (GSRR-44), but the coder's reading of candidates cannot be kept out of the coder's mind.
  - No second person checks the numbers in the tables (GSRR-59).
  - The person who decides eligibility after registration is the coder, who knows the rules (GSRR-33). The fixed order, the AI screener's independent judgements and the released decisions show this influence but do not remove it.
  - The exploration set was used to build the rules and for calibration, and is then coded again; its tables are calibration material (GSRR-39).
  - Codebook version 2 was written after the exploration results were seen (GSRR-10).
  - C2b relies on printed items. Instruments whose items are not printed are coded "items unavailable", and the instruments with printed items may differ systematically from the others.
