\Ssec{1}{Literature searches and prior evaluation designs}

\paragraph{Search record.} Three rounds of searches were made in November 2026, each split into sub-questions that were searched in parallel with AI-assisted search; every statement used in the paper was then checked by the author against the source's text (full text unless marked ``abstract'' below).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Comparisons behind reported per-person gains.} Sub-questions: the baselines of studies in which a personalized model beats a general one; choosing structure and shrinkage on other people; sequence models against simple baselines, including searches for evidence that per-person gains survive. Fifteen new sources were registered and twelve claims were rated by three independent readings (6 confirmed, 3 confirmed with reservations, 3 undecided). Full texts were obtained from Europe PMC, PubMed Central, arXiv, JMLR, EDM proceedings and authors' pages. No study was found that chooses the shrinkage on other people with the full-pooling limit among the candidates and compares with a shared model of the same structure, nor one that compares an HMM with a first-order Markov chain by predictive likelihood on behavioural symbol sequences.
\item \emph{Prior art for shrinkage toward a shared model.} Sub-questions: multilevel and mixed HMMs and MAP speaker adaptation; hierarchical and empirical-Bayes pooling and variance estimates at zero; personalized input language models; searches for counter-evidence. Twenty-four new sources were registered; fourteen claims were rated (6 confirmed, 5 confirmed with reservations, 1 undecided, 2 rejected). Full texts were obtained from the ACL Anthology, the ISCA archive, PsyArXiv/OSF, arXiv, bioRxiv, Europe PMC, EDM proceedings and authors' pages. A within-data comparison of an HMM with a first-order chain was found \citep{Shirley2010}; a comparison on later data of the same person was not. The search for clinical experience-sampling studies was run once and its coverage is limited.
\item \emph{Keystroke user models and cold start.} Sources recorded for this round: OpenAlex, arXiv, the ACL Anthology, medRxiv, EDM proceedings, a Japanese educational-technology journal and general web search. No study was found that fits per-person sequence models to keystroke symbols and compares them with the person's own first-order chain and with a shared model chosen on other people; the nearest work is \citet{Uto2020} (Table~\ref{tab:prior}).
\end{enumerate}
The rounds recorded the sub-questions, the registered sources and the verification of each claim, but not the exact query strings or the number of records screened. The statement on \citet{LiuMeng2016} was checked against the arXiv version (arXiv:1510.08539v1); the journal version's abstract, the part we could access, states the same trade-off between relevance and robustness.

\paragraph{Recorded search.} To make the statement in the introduction checkable, one further search was run on 20 November 2026 with the query strings, dates and counts recorded (Table~\ref{tab:search}). It asked for studies that fit per-person sequence models to keystroke behaviour and compare them, on the person's later data, with the person's own simple baseline or with a shared model. Within these searches no such study was found. The nearest were \citet{Uto2020}, who compare learner-dependent and shared HMMs on the same data (Table~\ref{tab:prior}), and \citet{Monaco2018}, who evaluate per-user HMMs on later sessions of the same users but compare them with other per-user models for identification and verification, not with the person's own simple baseline or a shared model. Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science, the ACM Digital Library and IEEE Xplore were not searched; EDM proceedings pages were screened by title only, and OpenAlex covers the EDM conference incompletely. ``Not found'' therefore means not found in these searches.

\Ssec{3}{Symbolization and data sources}

\paragraph{Features.} Every second, over the key events of the preceding 30\,s: $f_1$ median flight time (press minus previous release, kept if below 2,000\,ms; 0 if none); $f_3$ share of presses that are deletions; $f_4$ mean burst length (a burst continues while the gap from the previous release is below 200\,ms); $f_5$ number of gaps of at least 2,000\,ms between consecutive presses; $f_6$ share of deletions followed by such a gap. A release is assumed 100\,ms after each press, or at the next press if earlier. Navigation, modifier and function keys are ignored; the buffer holds at most 600 events.

\paragraph{Composite values.} With $\varphi(x)=\min(1,\max(0,(x-\beta)/(\kappa\beta)))$, $\kappa=2$ and $\beta=150$, 0.06, 5.0, 2.0 and 0.08 for $f_1,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6$:
\[ x=\varphi(f_3)\cdot0.30+\varphi(f_6)\cdot0.25+\varphi(f_1)\cdot0.25+\varphi(f_5)\cdot0.20,\qquad
 y=\varphi(f_4)\cdot0.40+(1-\varphi(f_1))\cdot0.35+(1-\varphi(f_5))\cdot0.25, \]
both clipped to $[0,1]$. The first weights deletions, deletions followed by a pause, flight time and pauses; the second weights burst length, short flight times and few pauses. Neither is read as a state.

\paragraph{Smoothing and pauses.} During typing (a tick with $f_1>0$ and less than 2\,s of silence) each value is smoothed as $e\leftarrow0.3\,\text{raw}+0.7\,e$; during pauses the smoothing weight is 0.15, 0.18 or 0.25 after 0, 15 or 25\,s of silence. Initial values are $x=0.1$, $y=0.8$. In pauses the features are replaced by fixed functions of the silence, and the typing context is reset after a gap of more than 10\,s. All constants are fixed and published with the kit.

\paragraph{Grid and the deletion-run symbol.} Each smoothed value is cut into five equal bins, and the symbol is $5\,x_{\text{bin}}+y_{\text{bin}}$ (0 to 24). A run of 14 or more consecutive deletions replaces the grid symbol at the next typing second by symbol 25.

\paragraph{Kit.} The public kit contains adapters for one CoAuthor session and for one CS1 student-assignment (split into work blocks at gaps of 30 minutes or more), the symbolizer, the constants, the two variant symbolizations of S13, a self-test, and a conformance test that symbolizes 300 synthetic key-press sequences (fixed seed) and compares every row bit for bit with traces from the original implementation; the sequences contain no human data. The author's check on the raw data (CoAuthor: 1,157 sessions, 775,907 rows; CS1 2021: every assignment with at least two key presses, 177 assignments, 2,794,277 rows) found every row identical to the original implementation; it is not part of the kit. Instead, the kit lists SHA-256 hashes of the symbol sequence of each of these sessions and assignments, keyed by the datasets' public identifiers (CoAuthor session id; CS1 subject and assignment ids), with the 875 CoAuthor sessions with one observed interval, the ones analysed, marked; a script recomputes them from the public data and reports mismatches. Running the kit on the raw data reproduced every hash, and hashes of the symbols read by the analysis code matched for every session and assignment. The lists contain hashes only, no symbols or per-writer values. The kit uses the Python standard library only.

\paragraph{Data.} CoAuthor \citep{Lee2022}: the public release archive (SHA-256 \texttt{8d7380ca3811c671} \texttt{e774e43bfb9f5d32} \texttt{65e2a5d425788a06} \texttt{6a612831aa1e418b}). CS1 \citep{Edwards2023}: the 2021 release, Harvard Dataverse, doi:10.7910/DVN/BVOF7S, version 7.0 (keystroke file SHA-256 \texttt{950a587bb3a410ef} \texttt{c4aad0e64974d644} \texttt{2806345fddd13af7} \texttt{6676dfb8006a22b6}). Inserted text is used for its length only; no typed content enters any analysis.

\Ssec{4}{Models, fitting and scoring}

\paragraph{Parameters.} With $K$ hidden states: an HMM has an initial distribution $\pi$, a transition matrix $\Pi$ ($K\times K$) and emissions $B$ ($K\times26$); an AR-HMM has $\pi$, $\Pi$, first-symbol emissions $H$ ($K\times26$) and transition tables $A^{(j)}$ ($26\times26$ per state). The one-step predictive probability is $\sum_j p(z_t=j\mid y_{<t})\,B_{j y_t}$ (HMM) or $\sum_j p(z_t=j\mid y_{<t})\,A^{(j)}_{y_{t-1}y_t}$ (AR-HMM; $H_{j y_1}$ for the first symbol). Free parameters: 83 and 349 (HMM, $K=3$, 10); 675, 1,353 and 2,033 (AR-HMM, $K=1$, 2, 3).

\paragraph{Base smoothing and prior.} Base pseudo-counts are 1 for every entry of $\pi$ and $\Pi$ and 0.5 for every entry of $B$, $H$ and $A^{(j)}$. In the per-person fit the prior count of entry $r$ is $c_r=\text{base}_r+\tau\bar S_r$, where $\bar S$ is the expected counts of the last E step of the shared model fitted without the writer, divided by the number of symbols (each parameter group sums to 1). The M step sets $\theta_r\propto$ expected count$_r+c_r$, and EM maximizes the log-likelihood plus $\sum_r c_r\log\theta_r$. The multiplier $\alpha$ of S9 scales the base pseudo-counts.

\paragraph{Fitting.} Sequences are cut into chunks of 900 symbols for fitting (the state distribution restarts from $\pi$ at a cut; the AR-HMM keeps the true previous symbol); scoring is not chunked. The shared model of each structure is fitted to all writers' non-held-out units from ten starting points (Dirichlet(1) draws; for the AR-HMM, pooled first-symbol frequencies and a perturbed pooled chain) and the best objective is kept; the shared model without a target writer is refitted from that solution. Each per-person fit runs from two starting points, the shared model without the writer and either a random start ($\tau=0$) or the writer's unshrunk fit ($\tau>0$), and keeps the higher MAP objective on the training symbols. EM stops when the objective per symbol changes by less than $10^{-7}$ or after 500 iterations; non-convergence occurred only at $\tau=0$, and no decrease of the objective above $10^{-9}$ per symbol occurred. Changing the smoothing may move EM to a different local optimum; this was not separated from the effect of refitting. The shared model refitted without a target writer starts from a solution that used that writer's training units, which may steer the optimum it reaches.

\paragraph{Scoring.} Each held-out CoAuthor session and each CS1 work block is a sequence, scored one step ahead from $\pi$. The first symbol is scored by $\pi B$ (HMM) or $\pi H$ (AR-HMM), and by the writer's first-symbol frequencies plus 0.5 for the own chain, whose transition counts also have 0.5 added. First symbols are counted in the per-symbol average. They are about 0.15\% of held-out symbols (median over writers). Scoring without them (post hoc; CoAuthor 15-session panel; the choice unchanged) gives:

\paragraph{Choice objectives and ties.} For a target writer, the joint choice takes the (structure, $\tau$) pair with the best median, over the other writers of the panel and $k$, of per-person minus own chain on their held-out units; because a writer's own chain does not depend on the candidate, this is the median of each writer's held-out log-likelihood centred on their own chain. In the fixed-structure rows, $\tau$ is chosen by the median of per-person minus same-structure shared, so a writer for whom the shared model itself ($\tau=\infty$) is chosen has a difference of zero; in the joint choice no candidate gives zero against the own chain. The two objectives can pick different $\tau$ for the same structure: in the 15-session panel at $k=15$ the joint choice gives $3\times10^5$ to 16 of 17 writers and the fixed \arthree{} row $10^6$ to all. Ties favour the larger $\tau$ ($\infty$ largest) and, between structures, fewer free parameters. The objectives are not the target writer's accuracy, and a chosen per-person fit can lose to the shared model on the target.

\Ssec{5}{Candidate grids and their history}

The final grid of $\tau$ is $\{0, 10^2, 10^3, 10^4, 3\times10^4, 10^5, 3\times10^5, 10^6, \infty\}$, where $\infty$ is the shared model itself without fitting, and the structures are the \hmmthree{} and the \arone{} to \arthree{} (36 candidate pairs). The grids were extended after seeing results (S8):
\begin{enumerate}
\item The first analysis fitted a \hmmthree{} per writer, without shrinkage, against a shared model and the own chain.
\item The second analysis used HMMs with 3, 6 and 10 states and AR-HMMs with 1 to 3 states, and $\tau\in\{0,10^2,10^3,10^4\}$. The chosen $\tau$ for the AR-HMMs was $10^4$, the top of the grid, for nearly all writers.
\item The third analysis extended the grid to $10^6$ and $\infty$ after seeing this, kept the \hmmthree{} and the AR-HMMs, and added the joint choice. Cells common to both grids were reproduced exactly. With the extended grid, CS1 chose $\infty$ for all writers in the AR-HMMs (except the \arone{} at $k=5$), and CoAuthor chose $10^6$ for the AR-HMMs at small $k$.
\end{enumerate}
The structure grid stops at three states for the AR-HMM; the \arthree{} was chosen by the joint choice for every writer in every cell.

\Ssec{6}{Prior mode, smoothing effect, the mean objective and the pause share}

\paragraph{Prior mode and smoothing effect.} The prior mode is the normalized $\bar S$ without the base smoothing, the limit of the per-person fit as $\tau\to\infty$. It is not a candidate in the main protocol and is not used as a stand-alone diagnostic of per-person value. The smoothing effect is the median of prior mode minus shared on held-out symbols; it does not depend on $k$.

\Ssec{7}{Intervals and interquartile ranges}

Bootstrap intervals of the median resample writers with replacement 2,000 times with their values fixed and take the 2.5\% and 97.5\% points; quartiles are computed with the inverted empirical distribution function. With 17 writers the two coincide in almost every cell (2,489 of 2,512 cells in the second analysis): the median of a resample of 17 is its 9th order statistic, whose 2.5\% and 97.5\% points fall on the 5th and 13th order statistics of the original values (the probability that the resampled median is at or below the 4th is 0.008 and at or below the 5th 0.036), which are also the quartiles. This is why, in the 15-session panel, the intervals of Table~2 of the paper and of the symmetric refit in Table~3 equal the quartiles; it is not an error of aggregation. \Ssec{8}{Status of the analyses}

For every analysis and check, the conditions (rows, grids, readings, and how each outcome would change the text) were committed to a version-controlled repository before its code or, for reanalyses of stored values, before its computation. Each run recorded the code version, the input hashes and a self-test; mutation tests checked that the self-tests fail when the code is broken. The four analyses, designed one after another:
\begin{enumerate}
\item per-person \hmmthree{} against a shared model and the own chain (CoAuthor);
\item structures (HMM with 3, 6, 10 states; AR-HMM with 1 to 3) and shrinkage $\tau\le10^4$ chosen on other writers (CoAuthor and CS1);
\item the extended grid of $\tau$ and the joint choice;
\item the prior mode and the smoothing effect.
\end{enumerate}
The twelve later checks, counted as listed:
\begin{enumerate}
\item unsmoothed and non-overlapping symbolizations with pseudo-residuals (S13);
\item structure compensation (S12);
\item synthetic sensitivity, first null (S10);
\item the prior-mode decomposition applied to synthetic writers (S10);
\item ten seeds per level and false-alarm rates for the first null (S10);
\item the prior mode as a candidate (S6);
\item the choice by the mean (S6);
\item the pause share (S6);
\item the writer split (S9);
\item the smoothing checks: the base smoothing and the own chain's added value chosen on other writers, the shared-only refit, the symmetric refit and its controls, including the data swap (S9);
\item the second null (S10);
\item the score without first symbols (S4).
\end{enumerate}
Six changes were made after seeing results: (a) the extension of the $\tau$ grid, after the second analysis chose the top of its grid; (b) the change of structure families in the third analysis; both bear on the main result. (c) The symmetric refit was added, and read as the comparison bearing on a designer's decision, after the shared-only refit had made the remainder vanish; (d) its controls, with the report of the chosen $\tau$, were added after seeing the symmetric refit; (e) the second null was added after reviewing how the first null behaved; (f) the score without first symbols was added last. Changes (c) to (f) are marked post hoc in the paper; the controls and the score without first symbols report $k=5$ and 15, the points fixed in their conditions. Readings were committed before computation also for these additions.

\Ssec{10}{Synthetic sensitivity check}

\paragraph{First null.} The generating model is a \arthree{} with transition probabilities between states of 0.97 on the diagonal. A writer's symbol-to-symbol tables are $C_w=(1-\lambda)C_0+\lambda D_w$ with $D_w\sim\text{Dirichlet}(2C_0)$; $\lambda$ is calibrated by bisection on 40 separate synthetic writers of 20,000 symbols so that the median over writers of the writer's own generating model minus the common one, on the writer's symbols, is within 10\% of the level $g$ ($\lambda=0.022$, 0.043, 0.090 and 0.191 for $10^{-3}$, $3\times10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$, $3\times10^{-2}$; realized medians \e{8.8}{-4}, \e{2.96}{-3}, \e{9.49}{-3}, \e{3.01}{-2}). Synthetic CoAuthor panels have 17 writers with 20 sessions of 600 to 700 symbols and 26 further writers with five sessions used only in shared models; synthetic CS1 panels have 23 writers with six assignments (two sequences of 5,000 to 8,000 symbols each) and 8 further writers. The joint choice and all fits use the fixed base smoothing.

\Ssec{12}{Structure compensation}

\paragraph{Question and design.} Does the per-person \hmmthree{} gain arise because the \hmmthree{} cannot carry the dependence on the previous symbol and the per-person fit compensates for it? Part 1 shuffles the order of symbols within each sequence (training, held-out, shared and own chain alike), which removes first-order dependence and slow drift but keeps the number of parameters. Part 2 scores other writers' unshrunk per-person fits on the target writer. The check uses symbol sets of 10, 26 and 50 symbols (grids of $3\times3$, $5\times5$ and $7\times7$ plus the deletion-run symbol), the cells 15-session $k=5$ and 15 and 30-session $k=30$, and CS1 $k=5$ read separately. Quantities: $G$, per-person ($\tau$ chosen on others) minus shared, on the original ($o$) and shuffled ($p$) sequences; $\Delta D$, the drop in the own chain's advantage over the shared model after shuffling (a difference of medians); $S_0$, the median over other writers' unshrunk fits scored on the target minus shared; $G_{0r}$, the target's own unshrunk fit (random start) minus shared.

\paragraph{Rule} (fixed before computation; 1/2 and 1/10 are wording cut points). Part 1 ``vanishes'' if in all three symbol sets $\Delta D>0$, $G_o>0$ and $G_p<G_o/2$, and ``does not follow'' if in any set $\Delta D>0$ and $G_p\ge G_o$. Part 2 ``others gain too'' if in all three $S_0>0$ and $S_0>G_0/10$, and ``specific to the writer'' if in all three $S_0\le0$ or $S_0\le G_0/10$. Shapes: A (vanishes, others gain too), A$'$ (vanishes, specific to the writer), B (does not follow), C (other).
