# Outline — per-person learning in sequence models of keystroke logs

Status: outline of claims, evidence and placement, revised 2026-11-11. Target: User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction (empirical research paper). Numbers are medians over writers from four sequentially designed analyses and two later checks (unsmoothed symbols with a randomized-quantile pseudo-residual check, Dunn and Smyth 1996; and a check of the structure-compensation reading). A synthetic check of the protocol's sensitivity, with its conditions fixed before its code, is reported in 4.3 and S15. Panels: CoAuthor 15-session panel (17 writers), CoAuthor 30-session panel (9 writers), CS1 five-assignment panel (23 writers); the all-writer panels are defined in S4. Model labels: hmm3 and hmm10 are HMMs with 3 and 10 states; ar1, ar2 and ar3 are AR(1)-HMMs with 1 to 3 states (ar1 is a first-order Markov chain). The shrinkage grid is tau in {0, 100, 1,000, 10^4, 3 x 10^4, 10^5, 3 x 10^5, 10^6, infinity}; earlier analyses on a grid up to 10^4 are marked.

## Take-home

In an exploratory analysis of keystroke sequences symbolized over overlapping 30-second windows, whether a per-person sequence model beats a shared model depends on what it is compared with: against a shared model of the same weak structure it can gain a lot, but once the structure and the amount of shrinkage are chosen on other people, what remains is at most about 1e-3 nat per symbol and is not read as a per-person gain. We suggest that studies of per-person sequence models of keystroke behaviour compare them with the person's own first-order Markov chain and with a shared model whose structure and shrinkage are chosen on other people.

## Contributions

C1. An evaluation protocol for per-person gains in sequence models of keystroke behaviour, and what it changes in one case.
- Protocol: on later data from the same person, place two comparison models (a shared model of the same structure, fitted without the target writer, and the person's own first-order Markov chain), and choose the structure and the shrinkage strength on other people, with the shared model itself (no per-person deviation, tau = infinity) among the candidates so that a choice at the edge of the grid can be read.
- Instance (CoAuthor, original symbolization): with hmm3 fixed, the per-person fit gains +0.188 nats per symbol at k = 5 training sessions (IQR +0.103 to +0.321; 15 of 17 writers favour it) and +0.165 at k = 15 (17 of 17) over the shared hmm3. Under the protocol ar3 is chosen in every cell, and in the 15-session panel the per-person minus shared difference is +4.1e-4 to +7.2e-4 for k = 1 to 15 (positive for 16 or 17 of 17 writers). This remainder is not read as a per-person gain: comparing medians (not a per-writer decomposition), it is larger in the gap between the prior mode and the shared parameters than in the contribution of the person's own data, which is negative (S9); and the gap between a finite tau of 10^5 or more and the shared model itself cannot be separated from a per-person gain. In the 30-session panel at k = 30, where 3 x 10^4 is chosen for 8 of 9 writers, the difference is +0.001 (6 of 9 positive) and is reported as a value only; so are the ar1 values with the structure fixed (+0.003 and +0.005 at k = 20 and 30).
- CS1: the hmm3 per-person fit does not consistently beat the shared model (medians 0 to +0.028 across k, at most 61% of writers favouring it), and under the protocol the shared model itself (tau = infinity) is chosen for every writer, so the per-person difference is zero by construction, not an observation.
- Compared with: the principle that selection must not use the evaluation data (Cawley and Talbot 2010; Varma and Simon 2006) and assessment of an individual by fitting on the other n − 1 people (Liu and Meng 2016, who note the cost in relevance to the individual) are established. Choosing interpolation weights on other users and testing on the same users' later text is the practice in personalized keyboard language models (Fowler et al. 2015; Shao et al. 2020; Adhikary and Vertanen 2023) and is not claimed as new. The per-person fit is maximum a posteriori adaptation toward a shared model, as in speaker adaptation (Gauvain and Lee 1994; Reynolds et al. 2000), not a multilevel HMM (Altman 2007; Aarts and Haslbeck 2025). Choosing tau = infinity (full pooling, which corresponds to a between-person variance of zero) on other people differs from a boundary estimate of a variance component (Morris and Lysy 2012; Chung et al. 2013): it is a predictive choice among fixed candidates.
- Novelty (as found in our search, not established as absent): applying this comparison to per-person sequence models of keystroke behaviour, and reporting that, when the comparison model's structure is improved, the visible per-person gain shrinks to a remainder that is not read as a per-person gain. The search record is in S1.
- Strength: observed in CoAuthor on the original symbolization (exploratory; the candidate families and the grid were fixed after seeing earlier results, see 3.4); CS1 cannot show a per-person gain under the protocol, because the shared model itself is chosen. The instance depends on the symbolization (4.3): with unsmoothed symbols in overlapping windows the remainder is also below 1e-3, but with non-overlapping windows the protocol picks hmm3 in CoAuthor at k = 5 and 15 and the per-person difference stays positive. Generality of the protocol is argued, not tested.

Supporting evidence (not a separate contribution): the person's own first-order Markov chain is a strong baseline.
- On overlapping-window symbolizations the per-person three-state HMM loses to it (about −0.5 to −0.6 nats per symbol in CoAuthor and −1.1 in CS1, negative for all or all but one writer); with non-overlapping windows (direction only) it beats the chain in CoAuthor (S13). Shrinking the person's first-order chain toward the shared chain (ar1, tau chosen on other people, 10^6 for every writer, so close to the shared chain itself) beats it by +0.043 at k = 5 (15-session panel, 17 of 17). The margin of the shared model alone (tau = infinity) shrinks with k; for ar1 in the 30-session panel at k = 30 it falls below the person's chain.
- Compared with: within-data comparisons of an HMM and a first-order Markov chain by DIC (Shirley et al. 2010); per-person keystroke-timing HMMs improved by conditioning on the key sequence (Monaco and Tappert 2018); autoregressive HMMs repairing within-state autocorrelation (Stoye et al. 2025); first-order chains adequate for web navigation at the page level but not at the topical level (Singer et al. 2014); information criteria choosing too many states (Pohle et al. 2017). What we add is the comparison on later data from the same person.
- Small per-person gains have been reported before (a person-mean baseline that neither personal nor general models beat, Aalbers et al. 2023; individualized knowledge tracing, Yudelson et al. 2013; Khajah et al. 2016); large gains have been reported against a single general model or with splits that mix a person's days across training and test (Weiss and Lockhart 2012; Taylor et al. 2020; Meegahapola et al. 2022); record-wise splits can inflate accuracy (Saeb et al. 2017), although which split fits depends on the use case (Little et al. 2017).

Supporting results moved to the supplement: the full decomposition of the remainder at very large shrinkage (S9) and logistic models on a proxy label (S10).

## Comparison models and use case (defined once, in 3.3)

- Use case: predicting a new person's later sessions with a shared model that was fitted before that person arrived. Each target writer is treated as such a new person: the shared model is refitted without that writer (leave one writer out), which simulates this use case rather than testing one fixed shared model.
- Shared model: the same structure fitted on all other writers in the dataset, excluding every writer's evaluation units (writers without evaluation units contribute all their units).
- Person's first-order Markov chain: transition counts from the person's training symbols with +0.5 added to every cell.
- Per-person fit: MAP estimate shrunk toward the shared model with strength tau. The sequence models have no person-level intercept.
- Choice on other people: the pair of structure and tau is the one with the best median, over the other writers of the same panel and k, of their own per-person minus chain difference on their evaluation units, ties broken toward the larger tau and then the structure with fewer free parameters; the target writer's values do not enter the choice. The target writer's evaluation units enter no fit and no choice; its training units do enter the other writers' shared models, as any other writer's do. Figure 1 shows which units enter each fit and each choice.
- Adjusted contrasts (not effects of one factor alone): structure (hmm3 against ar3, each with its own tau chosen on other people) and shrinkage (ar1 with tau chosen on other people against the person's chain, the same first-order structure). Both are rows of the main table; columns are named per-person minus shared and per-person minus chain throughout.

## Limitations (Section 6, stated once)

- Two datasets and one quantization for the main results, on overlapping-window symbolizations (4.3). Sessions and assignments are not days. Results are not extended to other languages, input methods, or other populations of writers or programmers.
- The hidden states are not given names and are not read as cognitive or affective states; better prediction of the 26-symbol sequence is short-range sequential dependence, not structure of mental state.
- No significance tests are used, and no threshold defines a meaningful difference; the structure-compensation check uses descriptive reading rules with cut points fixed before its computation (S14). Comparisons of medians are not per-writer decompositions.
- The analyses were designed one after another, after seeing earlier results (3.4, S12). The structure grid stops at three states and was not extended in this study; on overlapping-window symbolizations ar3 was chosen in every cell, at the upper end. Smoothed higher-order Markov chains and multilevel HMMs that estimate the between-person variance were not fitted.
- At large shrinkage, the difference between a finite tau of 10^5 or more and the shared model itself cannot be separated from a per-person gain; the remainder is not read as one (S9), and its decomposition was made only on the original symbolization. The 30-session panel has 9 writers, and its largest k shows interior shrinkage and larger values for ar1; longer records may change the picture.
- In synthetic writers matched in record length to the CoAuthor 15-session panel, the protocol did not detect planted per-person differences in transitions below 1e-2 nat per symbol at k = 15 (3e-2 at k = 5), and it returned only part of the planted gain (S15). The small remainder therefore does not show that smaller per-person differences are absent. The check plants one kind of difference only (in the symbol-to-symbol transitions within states; the hidden-state dynamics are shared).
- We did not compare with a shared model that includes the target writer's training units (use case in 3.3).
- The unsmoothed-symbol check removes the smoothing and, in its second variant, the window overlap, but not every source of dependence: the seconds since the last key press still carry memory across windows. Details and the pseudo-residual caveats are in S13; the structure-compensation caveats are in S14.
