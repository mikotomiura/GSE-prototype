To the Editor, Journal of Educational Data Mining

Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript "The 30-second signal changes of a hand-set keystroke state estimator before code-writing stalls
stayed inside a preregistered equivalence band against short pauses" for consideration as a research article in the
Journal of Educational Data Mining.

Work that anticipates when learners need help has mostly compared its signals with moments that include ordinary
typing, not with comparable pauses. The manuscript judges the run-up signal of one keystroke state estimator against
the run-up to short pauses by the same writer, with an AUC equivalence band set in advance and confirmed on a random
holdout of a public introductory programming dataset (CS1; Edwards, Hart and Shrestha, JEDM 15(1), 2023). In the
holdout (139 students), the AUC of both signals fell within the band, and the registered threshold table showed no
separation. Outside the registration, learners on simple quantities of preceding typing exceeded the estimator's
changes on the same pairs, and a calibration showed that the decision rule declared equivalence more often than its
nominal level with few effective writers.

I believe the manuscript fits the journal's call for comparative analyses and methodological rigour. Its contribution
is the comparison itself, which others can apply to their own run-up signals, together with the reference values of
the learners and the calibration of the decision rule. For replicability, the preregistration, the deviation, the
start records and the results are registered on the Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/mrkxt and
https://osf.io/uj6cy), and the estimator, the replay tool, the conversion, the evaluation and the explorations are
archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24026278.

Data. No new data were collected. The CS1 files were obtained from their authors on request, and I agreed to publish
only aggregate values, so neither the data nor the per-pause estimate rows are redistributed. The essay-writing
(KLiCKe) and co-writing (CoAuthor) data are public and appear only as descriptions on already seen data and in the
exploration.
