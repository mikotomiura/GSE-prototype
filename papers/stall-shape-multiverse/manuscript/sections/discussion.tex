\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The direction of the association between a keystroke label rate and essay score changed with the definition within
both detectors, and changed again with adjustment; the ratio of rates between a programming course and an essay corpus
reversed for most definitions; the structure of stall length in CS1 stayed. For researchers in educational data mining
and learning analytics who relate pause, stall or inactivity labels to outcomes or compare them across datasets, a
report should therefore contain the range of the conclusion across definitions and adjustments, with intervals, rather
than the point value of one definition. What generalises is the procedure; the score association is one worked example
on one corpus. Compared with earlier multiverse analyses, what moved here was of a different kind: an eye-movement
effect kept its existence and changed its size \cite{godwin2025multiverse}, and boundary pausing in CS1 changed
strength with the split at 2~s but not direction \cite{koutcheme2026keystroke}; here the existence of a stall--score
association stayed, and its direction changed.

\paragraph{Why the direction can change.} A rate of labels per hour is a count, and counts carry how much and how fast
a writer types as well as how often the writer stops. A writer-quantile threshold makes this explicit, which is why
the pause family with such thresholds correlated with the score at about $+0.6$. Prior work reports the same split between counts and durations:
pause counts can correlate positively with scores through text length, whereas pause durations correlate negatively
\cite{schaller2026keyscore}. Adjusting for speed removed the positive part and left a negative association for every
stall definition; adjusting for essay length left no negative one. These are patterns, not a tested mechanism; the
association that remains after either adjustment is the part of the rate that does not overlap with the covariate, and
speed and length may confound or mediate it \cite{mackinnon2000equivalence}.

\paragraph{Existing answers to arbitrary thresholds.} Mixture models, personalised thresholds, state models, location
classes and controls for typing skill (Section~\ref{sec:related}) each choose a threshold, a model or a control. The
diagnostic applies to them in the same way: vary the choice they make and report whether the conclusion moves. Methods
that keep the uncertainty of the choice, such as probabilistic assignment of pauses to components or averaging over
models, are the natural complement.

\paragraph{What the criteria established.} By criteria set before each quantity was seen: the direction of the
score association changed within both detectors (a lenient criterion, one definition on the other side of zero), and
the presence of an association did not move (S1); within-writer departure from a constant rate depended on the
definition; the CS1 length structure stayed. The reversal of rate ratios was counted against a criterion set after seeing point values, which
the two CS1 editions did not reach (Section~\ref{sec:status}). The strength of the association is described after seeing the results,
and the speed-adjusted values are exploratory. We did not test whether the definitional spread changes the performance
of a predictor or a decision about when to intervene.

\paragraph{Recommendation.} When reporting an association or a comparison based on a pause or stall label, report the
value of each axis, vary each axis over its range in prior practice and around the default, and report the spread with
intervals. As a working rule, report a direction only if the intervals of at least 90\% of the definitions examined lie
on the same side of zero, and otherwise report that it depends on the definition; in our grid this held for the fixed
stall thresholds and not for the writer-quantile stall thresholds or the pause family. Report the association with and
without adjustment for quantities that do not contain the label, such as typing speed and the length of the product.
The same applies to comparisons of rates between datasets and to the choice of a count model. The code is released
for this purpose.
