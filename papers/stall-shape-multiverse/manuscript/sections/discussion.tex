\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The association between a stall rate and essay score was present for most definitions and changed direction only
where a 15-s window met a writer quantile; on the grid of default and precedented levels it was negative or
negligible, and what moved there was its size and, among fixed pause thresholds, its presence. Adjusting for essay
length left no stall definition negative and most values negligible, whereas for pauses it raised half of the
definitions above $+0.1$. For researchers in educational data mining and learning analytics who relate pause, stall or
inactivity rates to outcomes or compare them across datasets, a report should therefore contain the range of the
conclusion across definitions and adjustments, with intervals, rather than the point value of one definition. Within
levels in use, our result resembles earlier multiverse analyses, in which an eye-movement effect kept its existence
and changed its size \cite{godwin2025multiverse} and boundary pausing in CS1 changed strength with the split at 2~s
but not direction \cite{koutcheme2026keystroke}; widening the grid to levels around the default also changed the
direction. The score association comes from adults recruited online, so its transfer to course data is untested.

\paragraph{Why the direction can change.} A rate of labels per hour is a count, and counts carry how much and how fast
a writer types as well as how often the writer stops. A writer-quantile pause threshold makes this explicit: its rate
follows the rate of key presses and correlated with the score at about $+0.6$. Prior work reports the same split
between counts and durations: pause counts can correlate positively with scores through text length, whereas pause
durations correlate negatively \cite{schaller2026keyscore}. The two detectors also answered the adjustments
differently: given speed, an exploratory analysis, every stall definition was negative but most fixed pause thresholds
were positive, and given length the stall association shrank towards zero while the pause association grew. These are
patterns, not a tested mechanism; the association that remains after either adjustment is the part of the rate that
does not overlap with the covariate, and speed and length may confound or mediate it \cite{mackinnon2000equivalence}.

\paragraph{Existing answers to arbitrary thresholds.} Mixture models, personalised thresholds, state models, location
classes and controls for typing skill (Section~\ref{sec:related}) each choose a threshold, a model or a control. The
diagnostic applies to them in the same way: vary the choice they make and report whether the conclusion moves. Methods
that keep the uncertainty of the choice, such as probabilistic assignment of pauses to components or averaging over
models, are the natural complement.

\paragraph{What the criteria established.} By criteria set before each quantity was seen, the direction of the stall
and pause associations with the score changed (a lenient criterion), the presence of the stall association did not
move, adjusting for essay length left no stall definition negative, $\varphi$ against 1.5 depended on the definition
in two CS1 cells, and the largest component of CS1 stall length stayed. The presence of the pause association, the
reversal of rate ratios, all sizes and the readings by class were judged after seeing results, and the speed-adjusted
values are exploratory (S1). We did not test whether the definitional spread changes the performance of a predictor
or a decision about when to intervene.

\paragraph{Recommendation.} When reporting an association or a comparison based on a pause or stall rate, report the
value of each axis, vary each axis over its range in prior practice and around the default, and report the spread with
intervals, with and without adjustment for quantities that do not contain the label, such as the length of the product
and typing speed; adjustment is an axis as well, since it can change the conclusion as a definition can. As a proposal
formed after seeing these results, sort each definition's interval into below $-0.1$, above $+0.1$, within $\pm 0.1$
(negligible) or imprecise (crossing a bound). Report a consistent direction or a negligible association when one class
holds at least 90\% of the classified definitions, report that the conclusion depends on the definition otherwise, and
give the number of imprecise intervals in either case, since a wide interval is a lack of precision rather than a
dependence on the definition. Because these shares depend on how finely each axis is cut, apply the reading to the
grid of the default and of levels with precedent and to the grid with the levels around the default added, and report
both (Table~\ref{tab:read}). The same reading applies to comparisons of rates between datasets and to $\varphi$. The
code is released for this purpose.
