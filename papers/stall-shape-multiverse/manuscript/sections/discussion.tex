\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The association between a keystroke label rate and essay score was present for most stall definitions, but its
direction changed within both detectors; among fixed pause thresholds its presence moved as well, and most values were
negligible. Adjusting for essay length left no stall definition negative and most values negligible. For researchers
in educational data mining and learning analytics who relate pause, stall or inactivity rates to outcomes or compare
them across datasets, a report should therefore contain the range of the conclusion across definitions and
adjustments, with intervals, rather than the point value of one definition. Compared with earlier multiverse analyses,
what moved here was of a different kind: an eye-movement effect kept its existence and changed its size
\cite{godwin2025multiverse}, and boundary pausing in CS1 changed strength with the split at 2~s but not direction
\cite{koutcheme2026keystroke}; here the existence of the stall association stayed, and its direction changed.

\paragraph{Why the direction can change.} A rate of labels per hour is a count, and counts carry how much and how fast
a writer types as well as how often the writer stops. A writer-quantile pause threshold makes this explicit: its rate
follows the rate of key presses and correlated with the score at about $+0.6$. Prior work reports the same split
between counts and durations: pause counts can correlate positively with scores through text length, whereas pause
durations correlate negatively \cite{schaller2026keyscore}. Adjusting for speed, an exploratory analysis, left a
negative association for every stall definition but a mostly positive one for the fixed pause thresholds, so after
speed is removed the two labels carry different parts of the rate; adjusting for essay length left no stall
definition negative. These are patterns, not a tested mechanism; the association that remains after either adjustment
is the part of the rate that does not overlap with the covariate, and speed and length may confound or mediate it
\cite{mackinnon2000equivalence}.

\paragraph{Existing answers to arbitrary thresholds.} Mixture models, personalised thresholds, state models, location
classes and controls for typing skill (Section~\ref{sec:related}) each choose a threshold, a model or a control. The
diagnostic applies to them in the same way: vary the choice they make and report whether the conclusion moves. Methods
that keep the uncertainty of the choice, such as probabilistic assignment of pauses to components or averaging over
models, are the natural complement.

\paragraph{What the criteria established.} By criteria set before each quantity was seen, the direction of the
score association changed within both detectors (a lenient criterion), the presence of the stall association did not
move, adjusting for essay length left no stall definition negative, within-writer departure from a constant rate
depended on the definition in CS1, and the largest component of CS1 stall length stayed. The presence of the pause
association, the reversal of rate ratios and all sizes were judged after seeing results, and the speed-adjusted values
are exploratory (S1). We did not test whether the definitional spread changes the performance of a predictor or a
decision about when to intervene.

\paragraph{Recommendation.} When reporting an association or a comparison based on a pause or stall rate, report the
value of each axis, vary each axis over its range in prior practice and around the default, and report the spread with
intervals, with and without adjustment for quantities that do not contain the label, such as the length of the product
and typing speed; adjustment is an axis as well, since it can change the conclusion as a definition can. As a proposal
formed after seeing these results, read the spread by three classes of interval (below $-0.1$, above $+0.1$, within
$\pm 0.1$): report a consistent direction or a negligible association only if one class holds at least 90\% of the
definitions, and otherwise report that the conclusion depends on the definition. Because the share depends on how
finely each axis is cut, apply the reading twice, to the grid of levels with precedent and to the grid with the levels
around the default added, and report both. In our data the reading gave one answer only for the fixed stall thresholds
over the full grid (Section~\ref{sec:res-adjust}).
On the grid of levels with precedent, the speed-adjusted stall association read as consistently negative, and the
other readings still depended on the definition; whether a reading is definite depends on the grid, so the two grids
should be reported together.
The same reading applies to comparisons of rates between datasets and to the choice of a count model. The code is
released for this purpose.
