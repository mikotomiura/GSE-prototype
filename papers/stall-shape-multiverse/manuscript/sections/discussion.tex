\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The strength of the association between the keystroke stall rate and essay score moved, depending on the definition,
from negligible to small-to-moderate (largest $|\rho| = 0.25$), and in the corner with the shortest window it turned
weakly positive. For researchers in educational data mining and learning analytics who relate stall, pause or
inactivity labels to outcomes or compare them across datasets, this changes what a report should contain: the range of
the conclusion across definitions, with intervals, rather than the point value of one definition. What generalises is
the procedure; the score association is one worked example on one corpus. Compared with earlier multiverse analyses,
the stall label moved further than an eye-movement effect, which kept its existence and changed its size
\cite{godwin2025multiverse}, and further than boundary pausing in CS1, which changed strength with the split at 2~s but
not direction \cite{koutcheme2026keystroke}.

\paragraph{The speed-adjusted association.} The adjusted results are exploratory, because the covariate was chosen
twice after seeing results (Section~\ref{sec:status}); as stated in advance, their direction also depended on the
measure of speed, although no measure gave a positive adjusted correlation (Section~\ref{sec:res-score}). The raw correlation leaned positive in the definitions in which faster writers had more stalls, which reads
as suppression by typing speed but was not tested. The covariation across definitions between the raw correlation and
the correlation of stall rate with speed follows almost algebraically from the formula of the partial correlation, so it
is not independent evidence. The same direction appears in the CS1 replication that controlled for typing fluency
\cite{koutcheme2026keystroke}. Speed was entangled with the writer-quantile family only; the fixed-second rates were
nearly uncorrelated with speed (median $+0.02$). The argument against fixed thresholds that slow writers have more long
intervals \cite{guo2020scenario} concerns thresholds of a few seconds; our fixed thresholds of 10 and 30~s are an order
of magnitude longer, and that argument was not tested in this range.

\paragraph{Existing answers to arbitrary thresholds.} Mixture models, personalised thresholds, state models, location
classes and controls for typing skill (Section~\ref{sec:related}) each choose a threshold, a model or a control. The
diagnostic applies to them in the same way: vary the choice they make and report whether the conclusion moves. Methods
that keep the uncertainty of the choice, such as probabilistic assignment of pauses to components or averaging over
models, are the natural complement.

\paragraph{What was established and what remains possible.} The four conclusions were counted against criteria set
before the quantity was seen [P, A]: the score association moves in strength and in one corner in direction, the
CS1--essay rate ratios reverse, within-writer overdispersion depends on the definition, and the CS1 length structure
stays. The direction of the speed-adjusted association is exploratory [E]. That such definitional spread would corrupt
the decision of when to intervene, or the evaluation of a predictor, and the mechanism of suppression are possibilities
we did not test. Agreement of rates does not ensure agreement in time: among the 63 pairs of a definition and a dataset
with a rate within 20\% of the baseline, 11 labelled mostly different moments (S3).

\paragraph{Recommendation.} When reporting an association or a comparison based on a stall label, report the value of
each of the four axes, and vary each axis over its range in prior practice (Table~\ref{tab:grid}) and around the
default, reporting the spread with intervals. Do the same for comparisons of rates between datasets and for the choice
of a count model, checking the direction of the ratio and $\varphi$ across the definitions in use
(Sections~\ref{sec:res-score}--\ref{sec:res-phi}). If most definitions agree in direction with intervals, report the
direction; otherwise report that it depends on the definition. The code is released for this purpose. Beyond this
recommendation, reporting the association both with and without adjustment for a quantity that does not contain the
stall label, such as typing speed measured in more than one way, may change how it is read; neither value is a causal
effect (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
