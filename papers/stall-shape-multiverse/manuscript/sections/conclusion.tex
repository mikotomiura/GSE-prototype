\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Across 89 definitions of a window-based stall label and 18 of a fixed-threshold pause label, the association between
the label rate and essay score moved with the definition: for stalls its direction changed only where a 15-s window
met a writer quantile, and on levels in use its size moved; for pauses the values were mostly negligible. Adjusting for
essay length left no stall definition negative and most values negligible, but raised half of the pause definitions
above $+0.1$. A rate ratio between two editions of a programming course reversed for a fifth of the definitions, below a
criterion set after seeing point values, overdispersion within writers depended on the definition in two CS1 cells,
and the largest component of CS1 stall length stayed. A study that relates a pause or stall rate to an outcome, or
compares rates between datasets, can therefore not rest on one definition or one adjustment: it should report the range
of its conclusion across both, with intervals, and say whether that range reads as consistent, negligible, dependent on
the definition or imprecise.
