\begin{abstract}
Pause and stall labels in keystroke logs rest on choices that differ between studies: a window, a threshold, a
condition for a stop and a segmentation of the log. We asked which conclusions drawn from such a label move across
definitions and which stay. We applied a window-based stall detector to four datasets released for research (two editions of an
introductory programming course, an essay corpus and a corpus of writing with model suggestions), varied its four
choices over 89 definitions around its default, added 36 definitions of a second detector that counts inter-key
intervals above a threshold, and gave each conclusion an interval from resampling writers. By the criteria set before
each computation, the direction of the association between the label rate and the holistic essay score changed within
both detectors: for stalls only with a 15-s window, a level without precedent, and for pauses between the 2-s and 10-s
thresholds, both in use. Adjustment moved it as much: given typing speed it was negative for all 89 stall definitions,
and given essay length, which correlated with the score at 0.75, it was negative for none. The ratio of stall rates
between a programming course and the essay corpus reversed beyond sampling variation for most definitions, and
within-writer overdispersion of counts depended on the definition, whereas the largest component of stall length in
the programming data stayed. Reports of pause and stall labels should give the range across definitions with
intervals; the code and the aggregate tables are released.\\

{\parindent0pt
\textbf{Keywords:} keystroke logging, pauses, stalls, multiverse analysis, introductory programming, writing
}
\end{abstract}
