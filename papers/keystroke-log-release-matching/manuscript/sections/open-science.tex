\paragraph{Searches for related work.}
The searches behind the statement ``to our knowledge'' in Section~\ref{sec:related} were made on 11 October 2026 (UTC), before registration: in OpenAlex we traced the
works citing the attacks on timings~\cite{gonzalez2021reverse,monaco2019searching,qiu2026keytar}, the rounding of
latencies~\cite{leinonen2017preventing} and KLiCKe~\cite{tian2025klicke}, and screened the titles and abstracts of the
works of four authors of keystroke-logging research on writing (663 works); we also read the full text of the
anonymisation survey~\cite{hanisch2021survey}. The search terms were not recorded, and Google Scholar and Semantic
Scholar were not searched.

\paragraph{Registered files and seeds.}
The plan, its parameters (\texttt{config.json}) and its seed (\texttt{SEED}) were registered before any data were
acquired, with the SHA-256 of each file. The present \texttt{config.json} differs from
the registered file only in three fields that the registered file left to be filled at acquisition: the version and
SHA-256 of the files acquired from each source. No registered value was changed. Every random choice uses a seed
derived as the first eight bytes of the SHA-256 of the registered seed joined with the use, prompt, writer, $N$, $k$ and
draw, so each draw can be reproduced on its own.

\paragraph{Data acquisition.}
We acquired the KLiCKe corpus on 12 October 2026 at 19:29 UTC, after the preregistration was registered at 16:43 UTC,
from the folder linked by the corpus repository at a fixed commit. We acquired only the archive of the argumentative
writing task and extracted only its logs, texts, scores and prompts. The demographic file, whose questionnaire asked
for the participants' Mechanical Turk worker IDs~\cite{tian2025klicke}, was not acquired, and the archive we acquired
holds no demographic file. The ENJA15 sessions were acquired the same day from the repository of the database that
distributes them, at a fixed revision (Appendix~\ref{app:enja}). The SHA-256 of every file is recorded in the study
repository.

\paragraph{Code and data.}
We publish the code that reads, processes and evaluates the two corpora used here, and the aggregated results, as a
release of the study repository with a DOI on acceptance. We do not write or publish code that reads other released
keystroke logs directly; in particular, no code reads the competition data. This lets others repeat and extend the
evaluation on the same corpora without distributing a tool aimed at a particular release (see the ethical
considerations). The data are not redistributed: KLiCKe, ENJA15 and the KeyTAR model are obtained from their sources
under their licences, and the repository records the versions and SHA-256 of the files we used. Results per writer are
not published, with one exception given in the ethical considerations.
\end{openscience}
