\begin{ethics}
\paragraph{Review by an ethics committee.}
The author is not affiliated with a research institution and had no ethics committee to which the study could be
submitted; no external committee or board reviewed it. The study collects no new data. It uses two public corpora under
their terms, identifies no individual and reports only aggregates.

\paragraph{The data and the consent of their participants.}
The authors of the KLiCKe corpus informed us (personal communication, 13 and 14 October 2026) that its collection was
approved by the Institutional Review Board of Georgia State University; that the participants, recruited through
Amazon Mechanical Turk, gave informed consent to the use of their data for research purposes and were informed that
their data would be ``used for research purposes by the broader research community''; that the data were reviewed for
personally identifiable information before release; and that the corpus is released under the MIT License. The
published description of the corpus states that participants received an informed consent form outlining the study's
objectives and procedures~\cite{tian2025klicke}. We describe the scope of consent in the words of the corpus authors
and do not claim that participants consented to any particular form of public release. The corpus authors saw no
particular concern with this analysis and recommended reporting the findings in aggregate and not disclosing
potentially sensitive or identifying information about individual participants. The participants of ENJA15 ``signed a
form in which they agreed that their translation data would be made publicly available under a creative commons
license''~\cite{carl2016lrec}; we follow the more restrictive of the two licence statements of the database that
distributes it~\cite{crittlicense}. We did not acquire the demographic file of KLiCKe, whose questionnaire asked for
the participants' Mechanical Turk worker IDs~\cite{tian2025klicke}.

\paragraph{The competition data.}
The competition data, which hide the texts, were not used, and we did not examine whether they share essays with
KLiCKe (Section~\ref{sec:threat-model}). Doing so would test a real release whose participants did not agree to that
use, and it is not needed to evaluate the processing. \textbf{[To be completed after the party is informed: before
submission we informed the party that released the competition data that this evaluation would be published and that
its data were not matched (date).]}

\paragraph{Weighing harms and benefits.}
The stakeholders are the writers whose logs are in the two corpora, the writers of other released logs (including the
competition data), the parties that release keystroke logs, and readers who could reuse the procedures described here.
We weighed the study against the principles of the Menlo Report~\cite{dittrich2012menlo}. \emph{Respect for persons:}
the participants agreed to the use of their data for research on writing; this study evaluates how well the release
of such data protects them, collects nothing new, identifies no one and publishes no result for an individual writer
and no excerpt of a text or a log. \emph{Beneficence:} the harm is that the paper describes procedures that link
processed logs to texts; the benefit is that parties who release keystroke logs learn which processing withstands
such procedures before they release more data, which protects future writers and the participants of earlier
releases. The attacks evaluated here are of known types (Section~\ref{sec:related}); what is new is their evaluation
against the processing used in practice. \emph{Justice:} no new burden falls on participants, and the benefit reaches
every writer whose keystroke logs are released. \emph{Respect for law and public interest:} the data are used under
their licences, their sources are cited, and the study and its analysis plan were preregistered before any data were
acquired.

\paragraph{Handling of the data.}
Logs and texts were kept on the author's computers and were not sent to any AI system or other external service.
Only aggregates are reported and published.
\end{ethics}
