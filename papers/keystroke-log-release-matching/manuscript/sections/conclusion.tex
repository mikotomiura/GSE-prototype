\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked how far each form of processing keeps a keystroke log apart from its text against a party that holds
candidate texts, and what each form costs the process measures of writing research. Against attackers that know the
processing, the masking used in one public release, applied to a public corpus of the same kind, left every essay
matched among 1,000 candidates. In the main analysis, the lowest matching on a form that computes process measures was
2.2\%, by halving the presses (median correlation 0.70), where the total length alone was the strongest attacker;
only forms that compute no measure came closer to chance. Stronger attackers tried afterwards as an exploration matched
more on most coarse forms, but not on the half suppression. Hiding the characters in this way did not keep the logs apart from texts held elsewhere. These are
measurements of the attackers and corpora tried, not bounds on the risk.
