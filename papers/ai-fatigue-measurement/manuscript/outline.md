# Manuscript outline

Version 4, 18 October 2026.

This outline fixes the structure of the manuscript, the evidence for each claim and the rules for reporting before
any record of the main set is coded. It contains no result value and no expectation about results. Each claim
names the item of the registration that defines it (GSRR numbers refer to the registered protocol, Generalized
Systematic Review Registration form), the output from which its values are copied, and the table or figure that
reports it. This outline is to be attached to the registration with its version, date and SHA-256, so the version
fixed before coding can be checked; any
difference between it and the manuscript is reported as a deviation (Table 2). The registration, its codebook and its
amendments take precedence over this outline wherever they differ.

## Title

TRACE: How studies of "AI fatigue" tie fatigue to AI, measure it and report it. A registered methodological review

The title is fixed with the registration and is not changed after the results are known. It repeats the registered
title (GSRR-1), with the article type in place of "Protocol for a registered methodological review". TRACE is the
name of the registered coding scheme (GSRR-1, Contribution 1); it is not an acronym and not the name of a measure
of fatigue.

## Scope of the claims

- The review describes; it estimates no effect and tests no hypothesis (GSRR-13, GSRR-56). No significance test, no
  prevalence estimate and no comparison between sets is reported. The bootstrap intervals in Table 4 describe the
  reliability of the coding; they are not estimates about the literature.
- Primary answers come from the main set only (GSRR-11). The exploration set, from which the rules were
  built, is reported in a separate table and is never used to state that a problem was observed in the main
  set (GSRR-57). The late stratum and the coverage stratum are reported separately and outside the primary counts
  (GSRR-39); the coverage stratum is never used to say whether a problem type is present. Tables of
  different sets, centres or population groups may stand side by side; no statement says that a value is larger,
  smaller, more or less common in one of them than in another (GSRR-12, GSRR-57). Proportions describe the coded set
  and are not estimates of how often a practice occurs in the literature (GSRR-63).
- A table based on fewer than 10 studies reports counts only (GSRR-39). A statement about a problem type is one of
  "was observed in the main set", "was not observed in the main set" or "could not be evaluated", each
  with the count, the number of evaluable records, the number not evaluable and the number unresolved (GSRR-57).
  Which records are evaluable is defined per problem type (GSRR-57). These statements report whether a problem type is
  present or absent in this set; they are not frequencies and not judgements that an earlier observation was reproduced. No
  other word of frequency or severity ("common", "widespread", "rare", "serious") is used about a problem type, and
  the words "replicate", "replication" and "confirm" are not used about the observations of the narrative review.
- The item-content types (C2b) are described and are not called problems (GSRR-57). C2b is a researcher
  classification of item wording. It is not a content-validity assessment in the sense of COSMIN, which asks
  members of the target population and professionals to rate the items (Terwee et al., 2018), and it shows what items
  ask about, not what scores contain (GSRR-65).
  It covers instruments whose items are printed; instruments with items unavailable are counted, and may differ from
  the others (GSRR-65).
- The level of attribution (C1) is described and is not a problem type; claiming "AI fatigue" on a weak attribution is
  counted through C6c (in the title) and C7 (ii) (in the main conclusion).
- C2d records whether reports disclose items, reliability in the sample, factor analyses and reasons for the choice;
  it does not rate measurement properties. C2d and C4 describe reporting and are not quality ratings (GSRR-55).
- C7 checks three conditions under which a conclusion goes beyond what was measured; it does not judge whether a
  finding is true (GSRR-57). The conditions compare different things: (i) causal wording with the design, (ii) the
  term "AI fatigue" with the level of attribution, and (iii) the construct term of the conclusion with the name of the
  main instrument. Each condition is its own problem type (GSRR-57), so the abstract, Tables 11 to 13 and the
  Discussion report them separately and never as a single quantity. Condition (iii) and C6a compare names, not the
  content of the instruments, and the number of records in which both hold is reported. "Within what was measured"
  means only that none of the three conditions holds; it does not mean that the item content of the instrument
  supports the conclusion.
- The coding rules were written by the first author (GSRR-4), who codes every record twice, at least four weeks
  apart; the second author codes a random sample of at least 20% of the main set, or all of it, once and
  independently (GSRR-44). Agreement within the first author and agreement between the two authors are reported
  separately; independent coding is claimed only for the records the second author coded. Both authors wrote the
  earlier narrative review, so their agreement is agreement between coders who share that view (GSRR-46).
- The main set is the set of studies included by 31 December 2026 after screening by the first author and an AI
  screener, each judging a record on their own within a stage; the first author decided where they differed, and had
  read the AI screener's answers on those records before the next stage (GSRR-33, GSRR-34, GSRR-38, GSRR-39). The AI
  screener was checked against human decisions on records that had been used to refine the criteria and that include
  more studies of centre-A and a different mix of population groups than the candidates, so its accuracy may be
  optimistic (GSRR-37). Only free full texts were used; records without one stayed pending and were not coded
  (GSRR-65). It does not rest on two human screeners; the screening by both
  authors after the manuscript draft is a sensitivity analysis, done knowing the results (GSRR-62). The set was drawn
  from one literature database built from free sources, and the first author, who screened it, knew the coding rules;
  before registration, the first author had screened 79 of the additional candidates after reading AI-generated
  appraisals (GSRR-34, GSRR-65). Human screening before registration was uneven across population groups; all
  candidates were screened again in an order that alternates between groups (GSRR-33). It is not a census of the
  literature on AI and fatigue. Every claim about the main set is about that set. How many eligible records lie
  outside that database are estimated from a random sample of 300 records found by one search of this review's own
  in three free sources (the coverage check, GSRR-21, GSRR-39); the estimate concerns that search, not the whole
  literature.

## Contributions

The contributions are stated around one question: when a study reports fatigue in relation to AI, how is the fatigue
tied to AI, and do its title and conclusion stay within that tie, the study's design and the name of its
instrument? The registered coding scheme answers it with checks of attribution, names and design, not with
validity evidence.

1. **TRACE, a registered coding scheme that links three things in each study: how fatigue is tied to AI, what the
   fatigue items ask about, and what the report states and concludes (GSRR-1).** Seven codes (C1 to C7) with their rules and a codebook
   for item content, fixed before the main coding of the main set; amendments made after calibration are
   listed in Table 2. Its reliability evidence is the agreement between two coding passes of one coder and, on the
   records the second author coded, between the two authors (see Scope).
2. **A description of how a set of studies not used to build the rules attributes fatigue to AI, measures it and
   reports it** (RQ1 to RQ5, SQ1, SQ2), with the problem statements of the registration (new points 2, 4 and 5 above).
3. **Item-level content of the fatigue instruments** (new point 3): for instruments first seen in the main
   set and whose items are printed, the composition by type of item content and, for reduced interest, avoidance and
   futility, the target of the item; the number of instruments with items unavailable is given beside it.
4. **Recommendations for authors and reviewers** that separate what existing standards already ask from steps that
   the standards cited here do not name (Section 5.3, Table 14). The latter are proposals that follow from the
   definitions of the codes; they are not findings and are not among the five new points.

The released materials (the code table with codes, item numbers and source pages but no item text; the screening
decisions made after registration; the scripts) are the means by which the tables can be recomputed and the scheme
reused; whether other people apply the scheme in the same way has not been tested. They are not claimed as new
findings. The tables can be recomputed from the code table; checking the C2b codes
themselves requires the original instruments at the pages given.

## Rule for the abstract

Fixed before coding. The abstract states the question of the review (Contributions) in one sentence and reports, for
the main set only:

- one sentence on the scope of the set, which is never removed: a set drawn from one literature database built from
  free sources, screened by the first author and an AI screener, closed on 31 December 2026, and not a census of the
  literature; with the number of studies and instruments coded and the number of candidates still unscreened on that
  date; if any problem statement changes when both authors screen the set (sensitivity analysis 5), or if that
  screening was not done, the sentence says so (GSRR-62);
- how the coding was done: by the first author twice, and by the second author for all records or for a random sample
  (with its size), with the agreement between them;
- the three things TRACE links, each with counts and no evaluative word: the distribution of the representative level
  of attribution (C1); for the instruments first seen in the main set with printed items, for each
  item-content type, the number of instruments with at least one item of that type, out of the instruments with coded
  items, with the number of instruments with items unavailable (C2b; the per-instrument proportions, which are the
  primary C2b quantity, are in Table 7); and, for each problem type of GSRR-57, including each condition
  of C7 and C6c, the registered statement with its count, the number of evaluable records and the number unresolved.

It gives no value from the exploration set and no comparison with it, and no value from the late stratum or the
coverage check (Table 16 reports the coverage count). It contains no recommendation. The
availability sentence names the per-instrument and per-study tables. If the abstract does not fit the word limit,
the steps are taken in this order, and only as far as needed: (1) the problem statements are grouped by code (C4,
C5, C6, C7), one sentence per group, keeping for each type the statement, the count, the number of evaluable records
and the number unresolved; (2) the C4 types are given in the form "observed: …; not observed: …", with the counts for
each type in Table 12, and the abstract gives the number of evaluable records for C4 as a range. No count or
denominator is removed for C1, C2b, C5, C6 or any condition of C7, and neither the question, the scope sentence nor
the C1 and C2b sentences are removed; (3) if it is still over the limit, the Background is reduced to one sentence
of at most 15 words; (4) if the journal's limit is below 250 words and the abstract is still over it, the C4 types
are replaced by one sentence that reports the number of C4 problem types observed, not observed and not evaluable
and refers to Table 12, and the C5 and C6 statements are given in the same compact form as the C4 types in step 2.
No count or denominator is removed for C1, C2b or any condition of C7. The C4 and C5 statements in Table 12 keep
their counts and denominators whatever the abstract does. A mock abstract built from empty tables (`abstract-mock.md`) shows that the rule fits 250 words
only with step 2 and compact wording (about 234 words), so the abstract is written in that wording from the start.

### 1. Introduction

- The first paragraph says that the review covers studies that measure fatigue in relation to the use of AI systems,
  whether or not they call it "AI fatigue": the term is used for constructs that range from fatigue attributed to AI
  in the items to general fatigue measured among AI users (centre B, GSRR-12).
- The question: how such a study ties fatigue to AI, and whether its title and conclusion stay within that tie, the
  study's design and the name of its instrument. A conclusion about "AI fatigue" depends on whether the
  measure ties fatigue to AI, on what the fatigue items ask about, and on whether what the report states is
  internally consistent and matches its conclusion. Item wording shows what an instrument asks about; it does not
  show what scores contain, which is a question of structural and construct validity and requires correlational or
  factor-analytic data. The eligibility of this review counts
  motivational and behavioural depletion as fatigue (GSRR-24), so items about reduced interest, avoidance or
  irritation are described, not treated as outside the construct. Overlapping samples change how much evidence a set
  of reports carries.
- What is known, from measurement research in general: a measure's name does not tell what it measures, and items
  are dropped, added and altered while the instrument keeps the same name; a methodological review is how such
  flexibility becomes visible (Elson et al., 2023). Studies of measurement practice in other fields count what reports
  disclose (Perrig et al., 2024). For AI-related instruments, systematic reviews have assessed measurement properties
  with COSMIN (for example, Lintner, 2024, on AI literacy scales), and a registered review of AI anxiety and attitude
  scales has no results yet (Jonaid, 2026).
- The gap, stated with the scope of the search: for internal consistency, 16 searches of free sources found no review
  of AI-fatigue studies that checks it study by study; for the level of attribution and for item content, the claim
  rests only on the reviews the authors had read (GSRR-10).
- The authors' narrative review and what this review adds (the section above).
- The research questions RQ1 to RQ5 and SQ1 to SQ2, stated as in GSRR-11 and GSRR-12, and the contributions. The last
  paragraph states the question of the second bullet as what the review answers.

### 2. Background

- Constructs: fatigue, exhaustion and burnout as the review's eligibility defines them (GSRR-24), including
  motivational and behavioural depletion, and why that definition shapes some outcomes (the share of reduced-interest
  items; the truncation of "no attribution" by the exclusion of studies without a relation to AI).
- Technostress and its exhaustion component; general fatigue scales adapted to AI; scales built for AI.
- Measurement flexibility and names: the nominal fallacy and undisclosed changes to instruments (Elson et al., 2023);
  where C2a (modification), C2d (disclosure), C6a and C7 (iii) (names) apply these known problems to this literature.
- Content validity in COSMIN (Terwee et al., 2018) and reviews that apply COSMIN to AI-related instruments (Lintner,
  2024): they evaluate studies that develop or validate an instrument, whereas this review codes how instruments are
  used and reported in studies that apply them. The unit of evaluation differs, and C2b is not a COSMIN rating.
- Reviews that counted measurement practice in HCI (Perrig et al., 2024) and for trust in AI, where they counted how
  many studies built their own questionnaire items and how many used existing questionnaires (Bach et al., 2022;
  Benk et al., 2024), and how C2a and C2d differ from their codes: a citation alone does not count as a reason for the choice in
  C2d. No count of this review is compared with theirs.
- Methodological studies and their reporting (Mbuagbaw et al., 2020; Murad & Wang, 2017; Long et al., 2024 on
  registration and reporting quality); recalculation of reported statistics (Nuijten et al., 2016) and its use
  outside psychology (for example, the recalculation of ratios and P-values from reported confidence intervals in
  medical abstracts, Georgescu & Wren, 2018).
- Only literature read in a free full text or a free abstract is cited; each citation is checked against its primary
  source before the manuscript is complete.

   - a step that this scheme makes explicit and that the standards cited here do not name, stated as a proposal (for

4. Limitations (GSRR-65 in full, with any added by deviations), including that the name checks of C6a and C7 (iii)
