\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Where the disagreements arise on macOS.} The macOS logger detected almost exactly the sessions that its own
rules specify for the presses sent, with almost the same durations. In the primary scope its rows agreed with the rows
that the rules specify with pooled recall
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.pooled.predobs.underlimit.recall} with live conversion off and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.pooled.predobs.underlimit.recall} with it on, a miss rate of
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.pooled.predobs.underlimit.missrate} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.pooled.predobs.underlimit.missrate} and a false-positive rate of
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.pooled.predobs.underlimit.falseposrate} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.pooled.predobs.underlimit.falseposrate}, and the limits of agreement of their durations
spanned \rv{macos-standard-live-off.predobs.primary.dwell.loa.span} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.predobs.primary.dwell.loa.span} over the speeds, with median absolute differences of
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.predobs.primary.dwell.mae.span} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.predobs.primary.dwell.mae.span}.
The comparison of the reference with the specified rows, which involves no observation, gave the same picture as the
comparison with the logger (Sections~\ref{sec:results-detection} and~\ref{sec:results-session}): pooled recall
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.pooled.gtpred.underlimit.recall} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.pooled.gtpred.underlimit.recall}, the same lowest strata, and durations whose limits of
agreement spanned \rv{macos-standard-live-off.gtpred.primary.dwell.loa.span} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.gtpred.primary.dwell.loa.span}, with median absolute differences of
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.gtpred.primary.dwell.mae.span} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.gtpred.primary.dwell.mae.span}. The disagreements of the macOS logger, and the long tails of
its duration differences, thus arise mainly where the sessions that its rules define differ from the sessions of the
reference (the three comparisons are read side by side, not combined arithmetically;
Section~\ref{sec:results-detection}).

\paragraph{Disagreements that the registration listed and those it did not.} The registration listed, as following from
the rules, a session that Backspace keeps open and carries into the next trial (S6), a session closed by Tab when a
predictive candidate is chosen (S10), a reading split by the minus, comma or period key, and, with live conversion on, a
session closed at the first of two Returns or Escapes. These set the lowest strata of the primary scope, S6 and S10 at
the fast speed (Section~\ref{sec:results-detection}). The prediction marks the first and the third as layers (a session
carried into the next trial, a closing key inside a reading), together with the trials that receive a carried session,
and these layers hold its lowest agreement (supplement). In the
trials outside every layer that the prediction marks, recall was
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.layer.none.gtobs.primary.recall} and precision
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.layer.none.gtobs.primary.precision} with live conversion off, and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.layer.none.gtobs.primary.recall} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.layer.none.gtobs.primary.precision} with it on, and the specified rows agreed with the
reference there to the same extent (recall \rv{macos-standard-live-off.layer.none.gtpred.primary.recall} and
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.layer.none.gtpred.primary.recall}). The layers do not mark the sessions closed by Tab in S10
or, with live conversion on, those closed at the first of two Returns or Escapes, so disagreements that the
registration listed remain among these trials, and this study did not separate them from any that it did not list.

\paragraph{The spread of the durations on macOS.} For the sessions that paired, the spread is not explained by the
timing of the logger's records: its key rows followed the injected presses by a few milliseconds
(Section~\ref{sec:results-reference}), and its durations matched those of its rules to within the few milliseconds
given above. Nor does it reflect how people type, because the input was produced by a program. The reference's
sessions begin and end with the calls that the input method makes to the field, which the rules approximate from the
keys. Differences in where the two place the beginning and the end of a session are consistent with the spread: for
example a session that the rules close at a key while the input method keeps the reading, or, with live conversion on,
the commits that the input method makes while the reading is typed, which would also be consistent with the larger
median absolute differences with live conversion on than off (Table~\ref{tab:session}). These mechanisms were not
tested. The beginnings and the
ends of the sessions were not compared separately; the two macOS cells used different plans
(Section~\ref{sec:method-plans}), so the two settings are not compared trial by trial; durations are compared only for
the sessions that paired, which differ between strata and speeds; and the delay between an injected press and the
input method's calls to the reference field was not measured.

\paragraph{What the interfaces allow.} Among the documented interfaces examined that do not depend on the application,
none on macOS reports a conversion, so the logger, which is meant to work in any application, infers the sessions from
the keys (Section~\ref{sec:observability}), and its sessions are those that its rules define. For the macOS logger the
agreement with the reference was thus set mainly by its rules rather than by their implementation (first paragraph of
this section). Rules that infer the sessions differently can be evaluated against the same reference on the dataset
without new measurement (Section~\ref{sec:dataset-uses}). On Windows, of the same interfaces, those that carry no text
report the windows of the input method, not the composition (Section~\ref{sec:observability-windows}), so the logger
records a period during which a window is visible, and its agreement with the reference, which records compositions,
includes the difference between the two constructs; the values of the two systems are therefore not compared
(Section~\ref{sec:observability-consequence}).

\paragraph{Windows.} The durations of the Windows logger were shorter than the reference's at every speed
(Section~\ref{sec:results-session}), and recall fell with speed also in S1, in which few sessions were split
(supplement). A window that appears after the composition begins or disappears before it ends is consistent with
shorter rows; a change of the window that the logger does not observe, and, on the cloaking path, an observed interval
that ends at least 150~ms before the row's time, for which the matching is not adjusted, are consistent with sessions
that fail to pair. Which of them acted cannot be told here, because a row does not record its path and nothing outside
the software observed the windows.

\paragraph{The definition of a Windows session.} Section~\ref{sec:results-detection} gives the ranges of the
per-stratum intervals and the pooled values under both definitions of a Windows reference session (deviation D12).
Within the ranges, the estimates of single strata moved with the definition: the recall of
\rv{windows-msime-tsf.defs.primary.change.recall.strata} of the \rv{windows-msime-tsf.defs.primary.change.recall.of}
strata that enter both, by at most \rv{windows-msime-tsf.defs.primary.change.recall.max}, and the precision of
\rv{windows-msime-tsf.defs.primary.change.precision.strata} of \rv{windows-msime-tsf.defs.primary.change.precision.of}
(\rv{windows-msime-tsf.defs.primary.change.onlyone.note}), by at most
\rv{windows-msime-tsf.defs.primary.change.precision.max}. The pooled merge rate also differs between the two
definitions (Section~\ref{sec:results-detection}).

\paragraph{The scales of writing research.} Writing research that counts pauses has traditionally set the threshold of
a pause at 2~s; the introduction to a special issue on methods for the writing time-course notes that what holds for
2~s, that processing on either side of it is likely to differ in kind, could be argued for ``any threshold a
researcher might care to choose between perhaps 250 ms and 10 s'' \citep{torrance2024}. On these scales, the key rows
of the logger followed the injected presses by a median of \rv{macos-standard-live-off.kt.obs.delay.all.median} with
live conversion off,
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.kt.obs.delay.all.median} with it on and \rv{windows-msime-tsf.kt.obs.delay.all.median} on
Windows (95th percentiles \rv{macos-standard-live-off.kt.obs.delay.all.p95},
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.kt.obs.delay.all.p95} and \rv{windows-msime-tsf.kt.obs.delay.all.p95}), and the signed
error of the intervals between consecutive rows had a median of \rv{macos-standard-live-off.kt.obs.interval.all.median},
\rv{macos-standard-live-on.kt.obs.interval.all.median} and \rv{windows-msime-tsf.kt.obs.interval.all.median} (its
percentiles in Table~\ref{tab:key-timing}), on the software path alone, to which
the path from a physical key adds its own delay, such as the sampling of a USB keyboard every 8~ms \citep{pinet2017};
and in the primary scope the durations of the sessions that paired, each as its logger defines a session, were within
100~ms of the reference's in \rv{macos-standard-live-off.dwellx.within100.range} of the
pairs with live conversion off, \rv{macos-standard-live-on.dwellx.within100.range} with it on and
\rv{windows-msime-tsf.dwellx.within100.range} on Windows, and within 1~s in
\rv{macos-standard-live-off.dwellx.within1000.range}, \rv{macos-standard-live-on.dwellx.within1000.range} and
\rv{windows-msime-tsf.dwellx.within1000.range} (exploratory ranges over the speeds; Section~\ref{sec:results-session}).
These figures are set beside the scales; how pauses computed from the rows would be classified against a threshold
was not evaluated, and the sessions that did not pair are counted in Section~\ref{sec:results-detection}.
